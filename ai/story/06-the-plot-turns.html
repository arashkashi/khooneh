Chapter 6 · 6 November 2026

The plot turns

Looking at the stacking diagram, the owner asked whether the small unit S would be better on the north side, and wanted that in the interview as two options. Then came two real plots to decide between, both on the southern side of an alley and both turned toward the north-east.

Turning point. Every plan so far treated the street as north and the yard as south. Measured from the owner's maps, the streets run about 40° round, if the maps point north. The street side then gets summer morning sun, and the yard side takes hot afternoon sun in June, more than twice the true-south figure.

What the sun study found

The two plots are about 240 m apart, so the sun doesn't choose between them. The neighbours do. With a building on the rear boundary, the first floor's December sun falls from 3.3 kilowatt-hours per square metre to almost nothing, while the fourth floor keeps 2.7. The AI's design put the parents and the high room on that first floor, so its strongest idea now depends on a fact nobody has checked.

Decision. S's side goes into the form as an open question, along with the choice of plot, what stands behind it, and whether the parents stay low. The AI's own answer keeps S on the yard side. The plans aren't redrawn until a plot is chosen.

The project's own notes held a surprise. A report from 2024 also compared two plots, plot 1 and a quieter cul-de-sac, so these may not be new at all. The form now asks.
