Chapter 20 · 7–8 November 2026

Walking through

The first walkthrough, a short film of the 1399 living room and kitchen in late October sun, changed how the owner looked at the work. They asked for one film per home, for both the 1399 design and the new one, now called 1405, plus films of the shared spaces, the roof garden and the sunken court with its birch. They gave one rule: keep that light, and add far more detail.

The light became the library's default: 15 October at four in the afternoon, the sun low in the south-west at about 17°, a clear sky, soft shadows and a baked bounce of light inside each floor. Every walk pauses at a main window while the sun runs through the year at the same hour, 21 December, 21 March and 21 June, with the date in English and in the Persian calendar. In the corner, a small map of the building shows a figure walking where the camera is. The facade got yellow Iranian brick. The rooms got door frames and handles that open as you approach, skirting, curtains, lamps, carpets, پشتی cushions, books, a samovar and real plants.

Decision. One film per home, entering from its own front door, rather than one long tour. Shorter films render at better quality and each family can see its own home. Films of the shared spaces, the roof and the court come separately.

The 1405 windows and details were drawn before the films, because a walk can only show what exists. The street side, facing north-east, got narrow upright windows in deep brick reveals; the yard side got tall windows with louvred timber shutters. The roof garden, the −1 shared room and the court were detailed in their own model.

Watching the first films, the owner saw what a plan cannot show:

  • A walk that ends staring at a bookcase should turn and show the rest of the room.
  • The kitchen under the 1399 stair goes nearly black as you climb.
  • A band like a wall seems to block the top of that stair. It could be a modelling fault or too little headroom; it is being measured.
  • The 3D map in the corner is hard to read. A floor plan with the figure on it would be clearer, showing both floors on a stair.

Each became a task for the next round, and every film so far is being reviewed for the same kinds of fault.

Decision. A wider view, 90° across instead of 79–84°, for all remaining walks. The eye stays at 1.6 m, so the walk still feels like a person; only the width of view changes. Wider would stretch the edges and make small rooms look larger than they are.
What it means. Rendering takes about an hour per film on this laptop. A script now renders all the walks on any computer with a stronger graphics card, Windows or Mac, and shows the progress as it goes.
