Chapter 11 · 6 November 2026

The owner answers

The form came back complete, and the notes in the margins said as much as the answers. Some of them were questions back to the AI: which side should the student live on, would a breeze really cool the home, are very large flats going out of date?

Decision. Homes that convert between one and two units are out. “If duplex it is duplex and if not it is not.” The AI had carried this idea as a safe way to stay flexible, and the owner found it pointless.

Other things settled quietly: one big home for five or six people, a family building that doesn't have to pay for itself, a rented student in the small flat, a real tree, a court wide enough to live beside. The feel came through clearly as well: brick, a small gateway, sun moving across a textured wall, water in the court.

Read together, the answers also pulled against each other. The high room was rated as wanted, yet nothing seemed worth the lost area. Parents should move up for winter sun, yet stay close to the street for old age. Three two-level homes don't fit a limit of 15 m² for private stairs. The AI didn't choose between these, and sent them back to the owner.
