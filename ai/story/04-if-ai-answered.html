Chapter 4 · night of 5 November 2026

If the AI answered

The owner loved the form and asked for it in both Persian and English, with cleaner questions. Then came a new request: answer the questions yourself and show what design comes out, on four floors above parking. The owner will answer separately, so the two can be compared.

Decision. The AI's answers stay hidden by default and never enter the owner's export, so they don't steer the owner's own view.

The hypothetical design

Parents live low on one level, with a 6.1 m living room by the tree, made by omitting one slab so the roof stays at +15.80. A family flat sits beside that high room. Above are a south-facing student flat and a big-family duplex, with its living floor at the top under the sky. The caretaker and a shared room face a sunken court on level −1. There's no level −2.

The critic's turn

Turning point. The first draft passed all 36 of its own tests, yet an independent critic found that five cars didn't fit, doors opened onto the void, the private stair was too short and the split left no real flat. The tests had never traced a route or a car.

The draft was redrawn. It now has four cars, a lobby straight from the street, a U-shaped private stair and doors that connect, and new tests walk every room from its entrance. The split gave way to a better idea: the third and fourth floors can become two flats of about 106 and 107 m², with no extra home and no extra car.

Some problems remain and are shown on the page: the high room's structure needs an engineer, kitchens are dark, parking is tandem, and the court takes most of the yard.

Next step. The owner answers the form. Where the answers differ from the AI's is where the next conversation starts.
