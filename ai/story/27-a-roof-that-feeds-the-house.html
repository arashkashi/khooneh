Chapter 27 · 9 November 2026 · done, awaiting the owner and the engineer

A roof that feeds the house

The roof had a pergola, a takht, two raised beds and a service yard for the cooling units. The owner asked for something else: "change the roof just for plants and gardening, a professional one", one that would "provide part of the food", with "spaces for harvesting and collecting" and "small sitting places here and there".

The AI measured the roof as a kitchen garden: the sun on every bed in December and June, the water a bed drinks in a Qazvin July, the weight of wet soil on a slab 16 m up, and the walk from every bed to a sink. The beds that get the most winter sun turned out to be over the big home's street rooms, a part of the roof the plan had not even counted as roof.

Decided (owner, 9 November).
  • A plot for every home, in rows of raised beds 0.6 m high: the first-floor home and the big home 6.8 m² each (the big home's with a children's corner), the caretaker 2.5 m², the small duplex 2.4 m². Three shared beds over the street rooms, 9.4 m², which the caretaker tends for everyone, under cold-frame lids in winter.
  • Herbs first: 60% of every bed is sabzi. In an ordinary year that is about 58 kg of herbs, a third of what the twelve residents eat, with 86 kg of vegetables and 54 kg of fruit.
  • A real work place: a potting and harvest station under a grape pergola, with a sink, a bench, a scale and a store. A grape arbour over the main path, a pomegranate and a sour cherry over the columns, an edible windbreak of grapes, goji and sanjed against the hot yard wind, compost, a drying yard, and two small seats, one in the winter sun and one in the summer lee.
  • Services move: the cooling units go up onto the stair-house roof beside the solar heaters, and their condensate runs to the rain tank at −2, which grows from 5 to 15 m³. Greywater pipes are laid now and capped, for later. The roof door widens to 0.9 m, step-free, because every bag of soil and every crate passes through it.
  • Three rules watch it: a plot for every home and a shared share (RG1), water within reach of every bed (RG2), and, as a hard rule, a load the engineer can accept, with heavy things over columns (RG3).

The garden adds about 18 t of wet soil, boxes and plants to the top of the building. That is the proposal's biggest cost, and it is the engineer's to accept: Iran's code is reported to ask 500 kg/m² of live load for a roof garden, more than three times an ordinary roof's.

What it asks.
  • Of the engineer: confirm the roof's live and dead loads, set the bed limit, and specify the root barrier, the overflow scuppers, the frost drain-down, the pump and the lift's headroom under the stair-house roof.
  • Of the owner: is the caretaker paid for the shared beds, or do they keep a share? And if the raised bay is refused, the garden takes its corner too.
