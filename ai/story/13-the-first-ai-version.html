The first AI version

The owner's answers pulled against each other in places, and the AI had sent the questions back. The owner's reply changed the rule: when answers contradict, research it, first in Qazvin, then Tehran, then Iran, then the world, write a new answer, and carry on to a first version.

Decision. Contradictions no longer stop the work. Research settles them, the new answer is written down, and the owner can overturn it.

The research was blunt. There was no evidence anywhere that a double-height room earns back its lost floor, so the hole in the slab went. A raised bay at the top keeps the height, if the city allows it. Moving the parents up for winter sun turned out to gain almost nothing unless they moved up three flights, and in Qazvin people get stuck in lifts when the power goes. So they stayed on the first floor, even though that overrides what the owner had answered. A rented gym, a public room and short stays each brought more trouble than a family building needed.

The AI drew the design and ran it through every step: checks, stairs, graph of connections, column comparison, sun, quantities, 3D. The sun moved one thing straight away. A high window on the west got almost no December sun, because the neighbour stood in the way. Looking at the renders showed something else: every render so far had been mirrored, left for right, and nobody had noticed.

Then a critic who hadn't seen the reasoning traced the first draft by hand, and found what the automatic checks had missed. On the top floor the shared stair came out onto a private terrace. Only the lift reached the roof. The main drain served nothing, and the caretaker walked in through the kitchen. All of it passed the tests. The stair, walls and rooms were redrawn.

The result is a study, not yet a concept. Sized homes, cars, heights and rules still need people: an engineer, the municipality, an architect, and the owner, who may well disagree with what research decided.
