Chapter 8 · 6 November 2026

Two quiet teachers

The owner named two sources of inspiration, to be used subtly: Peter Zumthor, and Hossein Amanat, the architect of Tehran's Azadi Tower.

Zumthor offers a way to talk about what numbers miss: materials that belong together, the sound of a room, degrees of intimacy, the light on things. Amanat offers a way to carry old Persian geometry into a modern building without nostalgia.

Decision. These stay as ways of thinking, not forms to copy. They enter as a short section of the form on how the building should feel, and any response in the design should be small: perhaps one material, a compressed way in, and one quiet moment of geometry.

This meets the sun study in an interesting way. If winter sun is scarce on the low floors, “the light on things” becomes practical: where should the little light there is be spent?

The form is also going further than the family. The owner wants to pass it to people who know nothing of this history, so it now opens with a plain introduction and a glossary, asks who is answering, and marks questions only the owner can answer.
