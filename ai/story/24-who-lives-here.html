Chapter 24 · evening of 8 November 2026 · measured; proposals awaiting the owner

Who lives here

The owner asked for stories: "let us build stories for this place … I think it would be cool if we could develop personas who will be using the home." Then they asked for more: test each home against an ordinary day. A school morning, guests for dinner, carrying a sofa up, a toddler, the laundry, the shopping, working from home, a cozy corner to sit in.

So the building got its people. They are made up. Nobody here is a real person, and they have roles and ages, not names. They are written down as data (personas.json), and each day is a scenario with checks the plan data can answer (scenarios.json). A new step in the pipeline runs every check at every build. When the plans change, the stories change with them.

The households (fictional)
  • A, first floor: the grandparents, 67 and 64. A retired engineer who walks every morning and uses the −1 gym three times a week, and a retired teacher who does yoga at dawn, cooks fresh food and hosts the family for Friday lunch. They chose the step-free floor for the long term, not because they need it now. On Thursdays a grandchild sleeps over.
  • B, third and fourth floors: a family of five. A father of 44 who drives to work; a mother of 41 who works from home on Sundays and Tuesdays; a daughter of 15 who plays the setar; a son of 11 with a bicycle; a toddler of 2 who sleeps in a cot in the parents' room.
  • C, second floor: the caretaker («سرایدار»), 48, his wife, 45, who sews at home, and their son, 17, in his exam year.
  • D, second and third floors: a young couple. A designer of 31 who works from home four days a week, and a nurse of 33 on shifts who cycles to the hospital.
  • Everyone: the stair and the lift, the parking and the bicycles, the −1 shared room, gym and court, and the roof garden. For a celebration, about 40 people.

The owner corrected the first draft. The grandparents don't use a walker. They work at keeping healthy, so their checks are about an active life: room for a yoga mat, the way to the gym and the roof beds, a store for sports and garden things, daylight where they sit. One light check stays, for the reason they chose this floor: if one of them is ever hurt or recovering, the home should still work.

A school morning in B

At 06:15 the mother is first in the parents' bath. At 06:30 the father and the eldest both get up and both want a shower: he takes the parents' bath upstairs, she takes the children's bath downstairs. Both are done by 06:47. At 06:45 the son finds his sister still in the children's bath, so he climbs the stair to the WC by the entry, where nobody is waiting. The toddler's nappy is changed at 07:00 upstairs.

Nobody waits. Three wet rooms for five people is enough. It works partly because the son takes the stair rather than wait a minute.

Night

From every bed there is a WC 3.3 to 7.9 m away that doesn't pass another bedroom. D's is the shortest, the parents' in B the longest.

Moving in

The removal van stops in the alley. The checks carry a three-seat sofa (2.0 × 0.9 × 0.85 m), a double mattress and a fridge from the street door to each home.

  • The sofa reaches no home. Even carried on its side, it needs 0.85 m of clear opening. The front doors give 0.72 m (A and C) or 0.77 m (B and D) once the frame and the open leaf take their share. The lift's door is not drawn; at the assumed 0.8 m it is too narrow as well. By the shared stair the sofa makes every turn, then stops at the front door. Taking the leaf off gains a few centimetres, not enough.
  • The mattress doesn't fit the lift car (1100 × 1400 mm), even bent a little and tilted. It goes up the stair to every bedroom.
  • The fridge fits the lift and B's front door.
  • Inside B, the sofa can move between the two floors: it makes the turn of B's own stair.

A toddler in B

  • From the kitchen the cook sees 97% of the living room and 46% of the family hall. That is enough to keep an eye on a child playing.
  • Every window's sill is at least 0.9 m, or the window has a fixed laminated pane to 1.0 m.
  • The stair is open on five edges, 9.6 m in all: the play hall, the passage and the family hall, plus the kitchen and the living room on the fourth floor. A two-year-old needs a gate at the top and the bottom, and guards everywhere else. The walk review asked the same (card P6).
  • The guard heights at the covered terrace, the terrace and the balcony aren't in the data, so they can't be checked yet. Every door is hinged, the owner's rule, so each needs a guard on the hinge side.

Who sleeps where

B was furnished for four people, not five. The beds are there: the couple's double bed and the two children's beds, and a cot (0.7 × 1.3 m) fits in the parents' room. But bedding, storage and the kitchen were sized for four.

The grandparents' week in A

  • A yoga mat with room round it fits on the living room floor.
  • The −1 gym is 16.5 m from the front door, by lift, step-free.
  • The roof beds are 13.5 m by lift, but the roof door gives 0.62 m. That is narrow for a man with a tray of seedlings or a bag of soil. Whether there is a step at its threshold isn't in the data.
  • The store holds 6.1 m² for walking shoes, the yoga mat, garden tools and seed trays.
  • The living room is darker than they would like: 13% of the sky, with the rear building built, and an hour of December sun.
  • If one of them is recovering one day, the way from the street to the bed is step-free. The bed is 7.3 m from the WC, longer than the 6 m wanted at night for someone unsteady. The bath has a shower, not a tub, with a seat, and there is a grab rail by the WC. But the doors on that way give 0.72 m clear, less than the 0.75 m wanted for a frame or a wheelchair, and well short of the 0.8 m that a home kept for old age should have.

Guests

Shoes come off at the door, tea comes from the samovar, and the meal is at the table or round a sofreh on the carpet.

  • B, 14 people at Nowruz: 16 seats on the furniture, or many more on cushions on the floor; 12 at the two tables, or 18 round a sofreh; a WC by the entry 6 m from the living room, away from the bedrooms.
  • A, 10 for Friday lunch: a table for 12; the guest WC by the entry.
  • C, 8 at the holidays: it fits, but the only WC is the family bath, 10 m away.
  • D, 6 for dinner: no table in the layout, though a sofreh for 8 fits. The only WC is upstairs on the bedroom floor, up the spiral, next to the couple's bedroom.
  • No home has hooks or a cupboard for coats at the entry.

Working from home

  • D's only desk is in the bedroom, where its sky is 9%. That is the room the nurse sleeps in after a night shift. She has no work place of her own.
  • B's mother can work behind a door, in the couple's room (14 m from the living room, 35% sky) or in a child's room while the children are at school. The play hall at the top of the stair has a desk, but the sound comes straight up the stairwell.
  • D's bedroom lies under B's living room (12.3 m² of it), so the nurse's day sleep hears the family upstairs. The caretaker's son studies under B's reading corner, beside the shared stair.

Laundry

  • A: the washer is in the bath, and the balcony is close, but it gets 1.3 h of December sun and 17% of it shows from the guest seat.
  • B: washing and drying sit side by side on the children's floor (the bath and the covered terrace, 3.6 h of December sun). But the parents' clothes come down the stair, up to 22 m with a basket.
  • C: no balcony, so washing goes up to the roof, 16 m and two floors away. The roof's sun isn't in the data yet.
  • D: the washer is in the kitchen and the balcony is beside it, but 23% of the balcony shows from the guest seat, and the bedroom's basket comes down the spiral.
  • No home has a basket spot, an ironing place or a folding table drawn.

The car and the shopping

  • From the front east car to the lift is 6 m, and to the stair foot 2.5 m. A reaches its front door in 10 m and B's living floor in 16.5 m, both step-free.
  • Every car reaches the lift in 10 to 12 m. That assumes car bodies 1.8 m wide in the drawn 2.3 m stalls, which leaves about 0.7 m between two parked cars: tight for a parent lifting a toddler out. The walkway beside the cars narrows to 1.1 m at the columns, tight for a pram and bags.
  • Every entry has a shoe cabinet whose top takes the bags. Every fridge has worktop beside it. Every fridge is a fridge-freezer, and no home has a place for a separate freezer. C and D have no dishwasher.

The shared spaces

  • 40 people in the −1 shared room and its bays get 1.5 m² each. The court is 9.4 m away, step-free. Tea is 6 m from the room. By lift, everyone arrives in about five minutes.
  • One WC for 40 guests. Where 40 chairs are kept isn't in the data.
  • The caretaker's round, from his door to the street door, the parking, the bicycles, the court, −2 and the roof and back, is about 200 m and 256 steps by the stair. No bin store is drawn near the street door.
  • The bicycle room holds 18 m² for three bicycles and two motorbikes.

Cozy corners

B has seven corners: the reading corner, the covered terrace, the foot of its stair, the two halls. A and D have two each. C has only its family hall. The shared spaces have the landings with seats, the soffeh in the shared room's west bay, and the pergola on the roof. No window has a sill deep and low enough to sit in.

What the people found (cards on the design page, for the owner):
  • Front doors and the lift door too narrow for a sofa. Wider leaves for A's inner doors and the roof door.
  • B: gates and guards at its open stair; sized for five, not four.
  • D: a table, a WC on its living floor, and a work place that isn't the bedroom.
  • Laundry: baskets on each bedroom floor, drying out of the guests' sight, and an ironing place.
  • Room between the parked cars and past the columns, a second WC at −1, a bin store, a freezer place, dishwashers for C and D, coat hooks at every entry, a cozy corner for C.
