Chapter 23 · morning of 8 November 2026 · proposed, awaiting the owner

A kitchen to cook in

A preview picture of the big home's living floor showed what the numbers had let through:

  • the fridge stood right next to the big yard window;
  • the television stood with its back to the glass;
  • one straight counter ran along the far wall.

The owner asked where everything should go, and how a modern kitchen fits the way this family cooks: rice and stews in big pots, a lot of frying, tea all day, and meals at a table or on a sofreh on the carpet.

Measured, the old kitchen was worse than it looked:

  • the fridge stood on the window line, covering 0.55 m of the kitchen's window, with the afternoon sun on it;
  • the sink and the hob both faced a party wall 0.6 m away;
  • the walk from fridge to sink was 2.9 m;
  • there was a quarter of a metre of worktop beside the sink and the hob;
  • there was less storage than four people need.

The other homes had their own faults. On the first floor, the hob stood under the window. In the small duplex, the fridge stood by the glass and next to the hob. The caretaker's windowless kitchen had no hob at all, and no way to get a hood's air outside.

Proposed. For the big home's fourth floor:
  • A preparation island with the sink, so the cook faces the family and the 4.3 m yard window instead of a wall.
  • The hob, the oven and a ducted hood on the solid west wall, where a hood catches frying best.
  • The fridge at the north end, by the way in from the entry and 3.8 m from the glass.
  • A tea station under the window.
  • A table for six to eight beside the island, at ordinary table height.
  • The television on the east party wall, at right angles to the windows, with the sofa facing it.
A bar to eat at was weighed and set aside: stools are hard on elders and children during a long meal, and the bar would have cost the living room a metre and a half.

The other kitchens were sized to their households:

  • the first floor, a home that older parents could live in, gets a U along the walls;
  • the small duplex gets one run, with the fridge at its north end;
  • the caretaker gets an L: the fridge in the alcove beside the riser, and the hob on the riser's face.

Closed cupboards almost everywhere, because of grease and Qazvin dust. Open shelves only for the tea glasses.

Rules. Eight new rules now check every kitchen on every build, each with a test that plants the fault:
  • the work triangle;
  • worktop beside the sink, the hob and the fridge;
  • no fridge at a window or beside the hob;
  • no window by the hob;
  • aisles, including round an island;
  • storage per person;
  • a route for the hood's duct to the outside;
  • no television against or facing a window.
All four homes' kitchens pass. Two televisions, on the first floor and the caretaker's, still stand at their windows and are listed as open.
What it asks of you.
  • Approve, or choose the plain L (no island) for the big home.
  • Decide whether riser R2 may carry a hood duct to the roof. The caretaker's kitchen depends on it; otherwise it needs a weaker recirculating hood.
Once approved, the Blender walks will show the new kitchens. They need to read the sink, hob and island positions from the data instead of guessing them.
