What is being decidedچه چیزی تصمیم گرفته می‌شود

The rule has two parts. Part 1: a room with a bed is at least 10 m² (owner-tunable, 9–12 m²); a smaller room keeps no bed, or grows. Part 2: a dry space under 10 m² that is not private has no full walls and no door of its own, and is still drawn, named and measured as a space. Exceptions keep walls and doors whatever their size: WCs, baths and wet rooms; technical rooms and risers; stores with locks; and any room that needs fire or sound separation (the stair, the lift, the plant room). An open space is recognised by other means: a step or raised platform (you like level changes with drawers below), a different floor or a rug zone, a lowered ceiling or a beam, a half-height wall or a shelf, a curtain on a track (you like curtains over small stores), a wide opening or an arch without a door, built-in furniture (a window seat, a bench, a desk niche), a pendant light, or a colour. They suit different places: the reading corner a raised window seat and a pendant; a play nook a rug zone and a low shelf; an entry a stone floor to the shoes-off line (no step in A, which stays step-free); a wardrobe alcove a curtain on a track; a work niche a built-in desk with a shelf and a lamp; the tea corner a tiled floor and a curtain over its shelves; D's dining a pendant and a beam. Single bedrooms of 7.7–8.6 m² meet B3 today; at 10 m² they grow, or (option C) become open sleeping alcoves, which suit a guest but not a teenager.
