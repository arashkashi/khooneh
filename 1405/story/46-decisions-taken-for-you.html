The story

46. Decisions taken for you

18 November 2026

On 18 November you said the questions had piled up: every time you asked for something it became more decision cards. You asked the architect to decide all of them as an independent architect, make the new design, and keep track of what was decided for you. This chapter is that round.

How the choices were made

Each waiting question was answered the way an architect would: usually the option that had been recommended, but checked again against what you had already decided and against today's plan. Some questions had been written before the small-rooms change of 17 November or before your answers on the play hall and the pipe shafts, so they were measured again. A few were settled by another answer:

  • A's bath keeps its tub beside the level shower. The recommended fourth drain pipe was ruled out by your choice of three shafts, so its long drains go to the services engineer instead.
  • Your earlier question "shower only or a tub too in A's bath" now has the same answer: a tub too.
  • The brick screen for B's street terrace is no longer needed, because the terrace became B's couple bath.
  • The fridge-pocket question in the caretaker's kitchen went away once the fridge moved to the hall.

Higher rooms

You wanted the highest storey a permit would still allow, not the lowest. No Qazvin figure was found; Tehran allows 3.60 m a storey on a wide street. The first, second and third floors are now 3.40 m floor to floor, about 2.92 m clear instead of 2.72-2.84 m, and the fourth stays 3.60 m. The roof rises by 48 cm, to +16.76. The ground floor stays as it was: making it lower would leave the cars too little room under the beams. The city must confirm both before the permit.

Stairs drawn as they are

The plans used to draw a stair as evenly spaced lines over its whole box, with no landings, and the court stair was missing. Now every stair is drawn and built with its real steps and landings. At the new height the shared stair has 19 steps of 179 mm a floor. B's stair box became 15 cm longer. D's spiral had treads only 128 mm deep where you walk; it now has 17 deeper steps that turn further, about 200 mm deep.

What else changed

  • B's couple bath is on the fourth-floor street terrace, with a tub, a separate shower, a WC and two basins; the old bath is a pantry with curtains, between the family hall and the kitchen.
  • D's upper floor is one open loft round the spiral. A door at the top of its hall keeps the bed out of sight of the open front door.
  • For the structural engineer: thicker walls round the lift and a concrete wall at the stair's street face, whose window becomes a narrow slot; thicker basement walls; the side walls 5 cm further from the neighbours from the third floor up, so the building can sway in an earthquake without touching them.
  • Windows reach higher, D's bedroom gets one wide window, the lower floors get pale finishes, B's balcony is 0.9 m deep so the bedroom below gets winter sun, and the window over the soffeh is safe for children.
  • Wider front doors, one dining place per home, the gap by the court closed for bikes, and the birch moved 35 cm further from the balconies.

Things that have no drawing yet, such as stair gates, cupboards, tiles and the house rule for the roof beds, are written in a fit-out list and shown under each decision.

The scores

The overall goals score went from 72.6 to 73.3. Light, baths and everyday life went up; services and cost went down a little, because B's new bath has long drains and the stronger structure has more pieces. B's play hall became smaller (9.3 m²).

Changing any of it

Every decision is listed on the decisions page and on the design page, with what was chosen and why. To change one, paste the line shown there into the chat, for example "Decision wip-st-core-box: option B".
