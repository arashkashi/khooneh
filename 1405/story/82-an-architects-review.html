The story

82. An architect's review

21 November 2026

The owner asked for an architect to review 1405 objectively. They wanted the ways and the furniture walked through, the doors opened, the shelves reached and the stores measured. They asked about windows that hit furniture, bathrooms with everything a bathroom needs, room for heaters, room for drying the washing, and the light. They asked for ideas from Spain and Japan, with Qazvin kept in mind. "Objective" was taken strictly. A finding counts only if it is a number measured from the design and set against a threshold with a named source. A fix counts only if it becomes a general, tunable rule, tested at both ends of its range.

What already held

Most of what the owner asked about already passed measured rules:

  • Every bath and WC has its bin, roll holder, spare rolls, brush, shampoo shelf and cleaners.
  • All forty hinged doors open fully.
  • Every storage front can be reached.
  • Every home has a summer drying place.
  • Every bedroom gets at least 1 % daylight.

What was wrong, and what changed

  • Store totals. The kitchen's mop, floor cleaner and tools were counted twice, and the second freezer was counted as shelf space: 511.5 litres too much in every home. Each thing is now counted once. The second freezer's food goes into the kitchen freezer's spare room, and daily kitchen things stay on the kitchen's floor.
  • D's clothes. They lay on a 0.4 m shelf with no rail at all. Now there is a 2.0 m open hanging rail behind the alcove's curtain.
  • Cleaners and food. In every home they shared a cupboard. Now they are kept apart. In the home with a two-year-old, the cupboards that hold cleaners get child latches.
  • Windows in the big home. Its kitchen worktop and bath basin stand just under opening casements, and a child can climb them. Both casements get child-safety restrictors. The bath window gets the satin glass its catalogue entry had promised.
  • Window hardware. All casements become tilt-and-turn, a habit from Spain and Central Europe. They air the room without clearing what stands under the window. The levers are lower, and every high hopper gets a rod to open it.
  • The grandparents' bath. Its shelves, hooks and mirror were set for a standing adult. They now come down to a seated reach.
  • Two doors. C's bath door and D's guest-WC door gave 0.67 m and 0.62 m of clear opening. Each now has a 0.80 m leaf.
  • Winter drying line. The big home's winter airer had 10.8 m of bar for an 11 kg drum. It is now 2.0 m long, with 12 m of bar.
  • The grandparents' store. It gets a second wall of shelves.

What was looked at and not changed

The review had said the shared stair's half landing crossed its street window. The stair's own geometry says otherwise: the half landing is at the far end of the box, and the window sits beside the floor landing. The new rule that guards against this passes, and no change was made.

What waits for the owner, the engineer and the permit office

  • The owner:
    • D's loft door, which a column narrows to 0.66 m;
    • A's living-room sill;
    • whether every door should give 0.75 m clear;
    • the lift landing;
    • C's kitchen, the only one with no window;
    • a small broom cupboard on D's day floor, so the mop and the ironing board no longer go up and down the spiral stair.
  • The permit office: widening A's kitchen window.
  • The services engineer: humidistat fans in the rooms where washing dries.

The rest of the review's list (wall clashes in the baths, roll-holder positions, the lift and roof arrivals, daylight factors by zone, curtains) is measured and written down. It is planned as the next round of rules.
