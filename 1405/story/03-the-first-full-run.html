Chapter 3 · night of 5 November 2026

The first full run

Before going to sleep, the owner asked for everything we had to go through the whole pipeline, with the open decisions collected in one form that could export its answers. No new design was written, because the ideas are meant to come from the conversation. The 1399 design and the note 09 draft went through every phase instead, as test cases that bring out decisions.

Blender wasn't downloaded, since nobody was awake to approve it. The 3D view and clay renders use only what was already on the Mac.

What the research changed

Splitting a home. I first called a later split risky, citing a court ruling that left a demolition order standing. The owner pointed out that a plan change approved before final approval is a different case, and that was right: the ruling covers a change made against the permit. The idea now is to design a home that can be one or two, and settle it with the city during construction.
Level −2. As a daylit room behind a 4.2 m court, it doesn't work: December sun stops about 2.4 m below ground. It's possible as an artificially lit shared room, subject to a site investigation and written rulings. The 2024 notes and the 2026 wish still disagree on its priority, which is now a question.
Windcatcher. Qazvin's summer wind comes in the hot afternoon, and its cool nights are nearly still, so a tower catching wind looks like a poor fit. The cool nights themselves are the asset. A plain shaft can be reserved cheaply if wanted.
Market. Population and household trends point to need moving towards 60-100 m², with 175-190 m² homes a thin niche. Against that, large homes are bought with existing wealth, and permits show no fall in size. No Qazvin household data or duplex sales evidence was found, so a local agent has to fill that gap.

What the pipeline showed

Running the same checks on both designs showed strengths and gaps. In 1399, three of the ten wet rooms above ground reach a riser, and its second-floor north rooms have no door to the core, which supports the owner's reading of one big home. In the note 09 draft, nine of ten wet rooms reach a riser, but thirteen doors open onto a 0.65 m strip that isn't a real landing, and the student unit has only 19 m² of rooms.

Next step. The owner returns the form's text file. Those answers set the specs, and only then is a new design written, as iteration 2.
