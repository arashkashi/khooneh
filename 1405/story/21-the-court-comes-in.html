Chapter 21 · 8 November 2026

The court comes in

The owner sent their drawing of the 1399 basement. Beside the caretaker's rooms, a green-hatched strip with planters runs from the sunken court into the building. They pointed at it: the green part is the connection from outside to inside and back, and they wanted it to be a space that goes inside −1, in 1399 and in the AI's design, 1405.

The strip was already in the 1399 data, but only as a planter strip, walled off from the court and seen through a window. It is about 3.4 m across and 2.2 m deep, under the parking. The brief that reached the AI had read the sheet's figures as 1.6 × 3.4 m. Those figures belong to the rooms around the strip, so the drawing and the scan were followed instead.

Decision. In both designs the court continues into −1 as a green threshold: a covered, unheated, planted porch, open to the court and glazed towards the rooms. The soil, the water and the drain stay outside the warm rooms, and the floor above gives it a roof. In the data it is a green room with its own type, so every tool treats it as open air, the page draws it green and the model paves it.

In 1405 there was no strip to open, so the AI looked for the place that does the same job. The line from the lift door to the birch already crossed the shared room to the court doors, so the threshold sits on that line: x 4.05–7.95, 2.0 m deep. A yard-face column now stands free at its corner, the basin lies in front of it, and the court stair lands beside it. The birch, the stair, the bridge and the line of sight all stay. All the hard rules pass, and every test passes.

The cost. The shared room gives up about 8 m², from 46 to 38 m², and about 30% of its direct skylight. With the rear building 6 m away, the glass that sees any sky falls from 7.8 to 5.0 m², and the glass at the threshold's back sees none. −1 already needed electric light all day, and it still does. In 1399 the strip was already under the parking, so its light barely changes.

Neither design can have a skylight over the threshold, because a ground floor and then homes stand above it. The engineer is left with:

  • the headroom under any beam over the doors;
  • drainage to the court's pumped sump, never by gravity to the sewer;
  • tanking with a root barrier under the beds;
  • insulating the slab's underside where it passes from the warm room over the cold porch.

The questions for the owner:

  • Should the threshold stay open to the court, or be glazed into a winter garden?
  • Which plants?
  • Would they give up parking or bike space for a lightwell?
  • In 1399, should the hall also open into the threshold?
  • In 1405, is the garden room worth the floor and the light it takes?

The owner clarifies: a garden behind glass

Later the same day the owner sent the drawing again, with a note: "the tree is in −1, and it is connected and goes inside the −1 via a little green garden. It is symmetric and could be surrounded with glass covers when going inside. So from −1 inside, the green garden is penetrated."

The AI had read the strip as a way in. The owner meant a garden that comes in. On the sheet, the court's bed with the tree, the strip of five planting rows inside and the stepped pit beyond all share one north–south axis. The strip is not a path to the court; it is the court's planting reaching into the house, seen from inside.

Decision. In both designs the −1 garden is a garden pocket: symmetric about the tree's axis, planted with the drawing's five strips, glass on its three inner sides and open to the court. People see into it and through it to the tree; they don't walk through it. A glazed door is there for upkeep only. This replaces the porch above.

Glass needed a type of its own in the data. A window is glass in a wall; this is a wall made of glass. So each level can now list glass panes. Every tool reads them the same way: a wall to anyone walking and to any furniture, but clear to the page's sight lines and a source of daylight for the room behind. The planting strips became spaces of their own, so no door leaf may swing over them. Five strips, 0.3 m apart, had been ruled out the first time because the paths were too narrow. That was right for a room to walk through, not for a garden seen through glass.

The cost and the trade. In 1405 the court door was a pair, 1.6 m together, on the axis. Now it is one 0.9 m door beside the pocket, opening in so it never sweeps the stair landing. The shared room keeps about 38 m². With the rear building 6 m away, the pocket's glass sees no sky. With an open rear plot, the shared room sees about 12% of the sky through it. The basin still stands in front of the pocket's west half, so moving it west is a question for the owner.

Still open for the owner: an open mouth or a winter garden; which plants; a curtain or frosted glass on the 1399 bedroom's side; one court door or two; and the basin's place.
