The story

85. What the render showed

22 November 2026

The first wide picture of the new passage on the big home's living floor did not look like the plan to the owner: "I guess the render of the passage on the forth floor does not match with the 2d drawings." A tall pillar stood between the private stair and the passage, and through the far door the family hall looked like a long, narrow corridor.

Measured, not guessed

The built model was cut through at 1.2 m above every floor and the cut set against the plan data the sheets draw. On the living floor the walls and doors in 3D were exactly the plan's. The pillar was the plan's own: a 0.5 m piece of wall where the passage meets the corner of the kitchen. The plans open two rooms of one home to each other only where they share at least 0.6 m; here they share 0.5 m, so a wall was drawn, a thin grey stub on the sheet and a full-height fin in the picture. It was what remained of the old pantry's south wall when the passage took the pantry's place. The "corridor" was the family hall's door lined up with the bedroom's door 2.5 m beyond it, seen from straight in front.

The stub is gone: the passage is opened to the kitchen by name, as the shared room's corner on the lower ground floor was, so it is open to the south over its whole width, like the stair beside it.

What the cut found elsewhere

  • The shared stair's small street window on the first to fourth floors was closed in 3D. It sits in the concrete street wall, which the model built whole, and the gap left for it was filled with a 0.25 m wall standing 0.125 m proud inside. The concrete now gets the window's opening, as a brick wall does.
  • The caretaker's walnut lattice screen beside the entry on the second floor was built as a solid plastered wall. It is now a frame with slats, about half open, as drawn.

A rule so it is measured every time

Until now the check between the plans and the model counted furniture only. A new hard rule, model.match-walls, runs the model builder's own wall planning without Blender on every build and compares, floor by floor, its walls, concrete, door openings, windows, glass and lattice screens with the plan: no wall in 3D the plan lacks, none of the plan's missing, every door and window open and glazed, no gap the plan draws closed, every screen open. Its tolerance (5 cm, from 2 to 15 cm) is the owner's to tune.
