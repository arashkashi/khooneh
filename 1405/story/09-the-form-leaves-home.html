Chapter 9 · 6 November 2026

The form leaves home

Until now the form lived on one computer. The owner wants to send it to other people, who should say a little about themselves, be able to stop and come back, and save their answers only when they have finished.

So there are now two copies from one source. The family's copy stays local and keeps everything. The shared copy leaves out the AI's answers, whose guesses about the family shouldn't travel and could sway people. It also leaves out the 14 questions only the owner can answer, and the street name.

Decision. “Finished” means every question has an answer or is marked “I can't judge this”. Most questions have no “don't know” option, so a strict rule would have forced newcomers to guess, and a guess looks just like an opinion in the results.

Progress is kept in cookies, as the owner asked, and also in the browser's local storage. A cookie is small and travels to the server each time the page is reopened, so the long notes may live only in local storage. The server keeps no answers.

Decision. Once people are answering, questions keep their identity and order, and new options only go at the end, so saved progress survives.

In the same hour the owner asked a larger question: how should the design be judged at each stage, so that it learns from the past and can survive times and conditions nobody can predict? The first proposal is in design/research/spec-framework.md: fewer general promises of flexibility, more cheap provisions that would be costly to add later. The owner approved it for now, with two changes.

Decision. The mix of homes on each floor stays a requirement. Only a massive advantage on the other side can outweigh it, and that must be written down.

The high room was harder. The owner wants it kept, and proposed drawing full floors for the permit and cutting them open once the building was finished. The AI advised against that route, because a slab holds the frame together in an earthquake, whoever benefits, and offered three others. The choice is still open. The owner also suggested learning from places with a similar climate, such as Madrid; that research has started, with the warning that Madrid's earthquakes are not Qazvin's.

Then came a new wish: balconies that seem to hang in the air, and that could grow later. The sun study gave this an unexpected use. At about 1.8 m a deck shades most of the June sun and costs only about a tenth of December's. Anchors for the full depth can be built in from the first day, while the question of when the deck may grow goes to the municipality.

The owner then asked whether the high room belongs at the top. It keeps its winter sun there whatever is built behind, though it gives up the tree and the yard. The owner also asked for the form to catch up with all of this, so it now carries the revised specs, questions about the high room and the balconies, and a question on which futures worry people most.

The research on similar climates came back. Madrid shares Qazvin's summer but not its winter, and its thick walls and patios assume almost no earthquakes. So it can teach shutters and night habits, and not structure.
