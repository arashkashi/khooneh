The story

59. Room for two columns

20 November 2026

On 17 November the appliances were sized to each household: the two larger homes got a twin, a fridge column and a freezer column side by side, and the two smaller ones a 60 or 70 cm fridge-freezer. You then wrote: "a new hard rules, each floor should have the capability for a side by side fridge and freezer please."

The place, not the appliance

The rule asks for the place. Every home's kitchen must keep a spot 1.2 m wide and 0.65 m deep with its back on a wall for the two sockets, 0.6 m free in front, and both doors able to open fully. A smaller fridge may stand there now. Putting a twin there later must not cut a worktop, narrow a way, or break a kitchen rule that the present fridge keeps. The last condition matters most. In the small duplex a twin fitted beside the old fridge-freezer, but its side would have come 0.1 m from the hob. That is not a real place for it.

What it changed

The big home and the step-free home already have the twin. The small duplex's kitchen is now planned round the twin's place: the hob, sink and dishwasher move 20 cm along the run, and the tea station goes beside the washer. Its 70 cm fridge-freezer stands in the place with half a metre of floor beside it. Nothing was given up in worktop or floor; the cook walks a little more. The caretaker's kitchen is too small. Every way of fitting the twin there takes worktop from a kitchen already short of it. The best place found was in the hall, at the kitchen's opening, and you chose it: "In the hall at the kitchen's opening (my recommendation): uses 0.78 m² of C's hall. The kitchen keeps its worktop, and the fridge ends about 3 m from the sink (the limit is 2.7)." The twin now stands against the bath's wall, facing the hall, with 1.2 m of hall beside it. The worktop on the bath wall reaches back into the alcove, and a narrow pull-out larder takes the old fridge's spot, so the kitchen gains 0.4 m of worktop. The fridge is 2 m from the sink and 3.5 m from the hob. The work-triangle check records that long leg as accepted, for this kitchen only.

"Each floor" was read as each home. In Persian a floor (طبقه) is also an apartment, and the duplexes' bedroom floors have no kitchen. A freezer upstairs would mean carrying food up and down the stairs.

Drawn so they can be told apart

The same day you wrote: "I can not clearly identify the washmachine and dishwashing machine in the drawing of the small duplex unit." The dishwasher sits under the worktop, and the plan, cut at 1.1 m, showed only its door line. Every appliance is now lettered as architects do it: WM on the washer with its door ring and control panel, DW on the dishwasher with its outline dashed under the worktop, OV for the oven under the hob, REF and FRZ on the fridges and freezers. The floor sheets have a key.
