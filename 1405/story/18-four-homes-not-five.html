Chapter 18 · 7 November 2026

Four homes, not five

Iteration 4 is the owner's new stack, drawn. On F1 there is a one-level home for anyone, step-free, so older parents could live there. On F2 the caretaker (سرایدار) takes the street side and the west of the yard, and a small duplex has its living room and kitchen on the yard. On F3 the big family has the children's floor, and the small duplex has its bedroom. F4 is the big family's living floor, as before. The basement holds no home now, and a small level −2 holds the plant room and the stores. Iteration 3's plain plan stays wherever it could: entry hall at the door, living room and kitchen on the yard, one couple room per home.

Decision. Four homes. The student flat is gone, and the caretaker moves up from the basement to F2. About 79 m² is the caretaker's, 111 m² the first-floor home's, 60 m² the small duplex's, or 70 m² with its balcony, and 158 m² the big family's, all as rooms without walls.

The small duplex was the puzzle. Its part of the yard is 4.6 m deep. A straight stair for a 3.2 m floor needs about 5.3 m with its top landing, and a U stair would have used about 11 m² over its two floors. The owner allowed a creative stair under about 8 m². A spiral, 1.8 m across, uses 6.7 m² over both floors.

Decision. A spiral stair joins the small duplex's two floors. To make it safe, the upper floor also has its own door on the landing. Beds and wardrobes come in that way, and it is the bedroom's way out in a fire. The big family's stair stays a U stair under the old 15 m² cap.

The owner asked where the washing goes. Most of it starts on the children's floor: two bedrooms, a bath and the towels. So the washing machine stands in that bath, and the washing hangs on a covered terrace a few steps away. From the couple room upstairs, the basket comes down the family's own stair. In winter the sunniest line is the top floor's balcony, one flight up.

Decision. The big family washes on F3 and dries on F3's covered terrace, out of rain and snow. It gets 4.5 hours of sun in March and 7 in June, but only about an hour in December. So the F4 balcony, with about seven hours, is the drying line in winter. The small duplex's washer sits under its kitchen counter by the balcony, because its shower room is too small. That breaks the owner's bathroom rule, so it goes to the owner as a question.

The basement became what iteration 3 promised. From the lift you step into a small foyer. Through the shared room's glass you see the tree, a silver birch the owner chose. A pair of glazed doors opens level onto the court. The court itself follows the study done alongside: its stair moves to the east strip, and the hoz becomes a stone basin outside the glass. The study's doors met a column, so they moved 0.35 m west.

Decision. −1 is a residents-only shared space with a small gym, and it is not rented out. −2 comes back for plant and stores only, under the street half of the building. An engineer must price the digging and check the fire exits before we commit to it.

The daylight tool changed one thing. A balcony on F3 would have left the small duplex's living room seeing less than 2% of the sky. Without it, the room sees about 8%. So F3 has no balcony. The sun still lives at the top: about seven hours on the big family's living floor on 21 December, and very little on F1 and F2.

What it means. A new programme broke some of the checks, and they were changed in the open. Each change says what the old rule was, what the brief changed and what the new rule is. One private stair became two. Five homes became four. A test that took fewer doors for missing data now credits open plans. The hard rules all still hold. Seven doors in open spaces have no wall to rest on: the price of fewer walls.
