The story

86. Risers right-sized

22 November 2026

The owner: "I would like you to review the risers their position and also their dimensions I have a feeling they could be too large, but I can be wrong." Then: "yes please make the corrections needed and also adding new rules and configs. but keep in mind do not make them too small and leave a small percentage for unpredicted things and changes. just a very small amount." And: "as you change the risers side, I guess some smaller relevant space would also need a review ... keep yourself open please."

How big is too big

A riser is a box of pipes standing in the same place on every floor. Each floor's pipes were already packed into each box (soil stack, vent, greywater, water, heating, ducts, with room between them and behind the hatch). The new question was how much length is left over. The measure: the box's clear length, divided by the shortest length at the same depth that still packs that floor's pipes, less one. A box keeps one outline on every floor, so its fullest floor sets its size.

  • The supply riser by the lift: 11.5% spare on the top floor. It is boxed in by two shear walls and holds most of the building's services. It stays as it is.
  • The west drain riser, by the caretaker's kitchen and the big home's stair: 69-75% spare.
  • The riser by the entries: 100% spare.

So the owner was right for two of the three.

Two rules

RS-snug (hard): on every floor a riser keeps at least 5% of its length spare. This is the owner's "very small amount", and it can be set anywhere from 3% to 10%. RS-trim (soft): on its fullest floor a riser keeps no more than 25% spare (15% to 40%). Both have planted faults at each end of their ranges. The old floor check that compared risers by area against 1.5 times their need had flagged nine levels. It now flags none.

Three designers, one judge

Three independent proposals came in: a snug fit with as little disruption as possible, a re-plan of the small spaces round the boxes, and a search for larger moves.

The larger moves were all measured, and none of them paid:

  • Folding the entry riser into the supply riser needs 2.00 m of length where the shear walls leave 1.65 m.
  • Without either drain riser, some drains reach no riser in time.
  • A boiler in each home would free about 0.44 m² per home in the supply riser. But it would undo the central boiler chosen that day, and it needs four flues on the facades.

What was built:

  • The west riser's north face moves 0.4 m south, on every level from −1 up. It is now 0.75 × 0.80 m, with 8-11% spare.
  • The entry riser's north face moves 0.3 m south, from the ground floor up, onto the landing's wall line, so no recess is left beside it. Its south face stays where the guest WC doors' leaves rest.

The small spaces

  • The big home's cleaning cupboard on the living floor moves 0.4 m with the riser's face and stays on its wall.
  • The caretaker's dishwasher drain is now about 0.6 m longer. The kitchen slab steps down 10 mm more under it (0.05 m), so its fall still fits inside the floor.
  • In the caretaker's kitchen the planner then put a tall pull-out larder into the new, deeper corner, 2 cm short of the room needed to stand in front of it pulled out. Only the hard rule caught it. The planner now drops any variant whose larder can't be used, as it already did for drop-down doors.

What it gave, and what it cost

2.69 m² of floor came back, and none was lost anywhere:

  • A: +0.48 (store, entry).
  • B: +0.92 (the children's nook and bath on F3, the cleaning nook and pantry on F4).
  • C: +0.48 (kitchen, entry).
  • Shared floor: +0.77 (−1's open middle, the ground-floor planter, aisle and walkway, the landings).

The entry riser still keeps 46% spare, and RS-trim flags it. The rest of its length is the WC doors' leaf rest, not pipe room. Cutting it to 10% would win 0.6 m² more, but it would leave 0.2 m recesses beside the box, which is dead space, and longer drains in B's top-floor bath. That stays an option on the card for the owner. One cost is real already: B's en-suite WC on the top floor now drains 6.1 m to its riser, against the 6.0 m recommended. The floor there has ample depth for the fall.

No hard rule newly fails. The goals score moves from 71.5 to 71.1, from that one drain.
