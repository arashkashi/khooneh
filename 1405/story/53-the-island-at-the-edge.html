53. The island at the edge

19 November 2026

You said yes to an island kitchen in the first-floor home, the step-free home for anyone, older parents too. The advice you accepted had four parts. The island stands at the living room's edge. The aisle beside it is at least 1.5 m, so a wheelchair can turn. The hob and the sink stay on the wall run. The island is for preparing and sitting, with knee room at one end at wheelchair height.

Why it had been left out

On 9 November the kitchen stayed a U along the walls: "an island would pinch against the living room's two columns". The numbers explain it. From the columns' east face to the front of the wall run there are only 1.70 m. A 0.9 m island and a 1.5 m aisle need 2.4 m. So the island can't stand beside the columns. It has to stand between them.

Where it stands

The two columns are 3.6 m apart, and the island stands in their line. Its kitchen face is on the old kitchen line, 1.55 m from the wall run: the wheelchair's turning circle, with a little to spare. It keeps 1.0 m to each column, the ring every island keeps, so it is 1.6 m long and 0.9 m deep. The way in from the living room runs past its two ends. A wheelchair comes in at the south end and turns in the aisle.

The line between the kitchen and the living room moved 0.9 m west, to the island's living-room face. No wall moved; it is the line between two open rooms. The island is the kitchen's edge, and it lies in its own room, so the 3D walk builds it (the walk places a piece in the room that holds its middle; B's first island was lost that way). The kitchen is now 13.3 m² and the living room 31.1 m², from 9.7 and 34.7. The open floor is the same.

What it is

The island is dry: no sink, no hob, no tea station. The fixture search had been free to put a sink on any island, and B's holds one. A's island is now marked dry, and the search leaves it alone. Its north end is 0.8 m of prep worktop at the usual 0.90 m, with cupboards on the living side. Its south end is a seated place 0.8 m wide, its top at 0.80 m, with 0.70 m of knee room under it, open both to the kitchen and to the living room. Someone in a wheelchair can work there, facing the room, and two people can sit and talk while a third cooks. The plans draw the step where the top drops. The 3D model still builds the island as one block 0.9 m high; the lowered end is in the data, but it isn't modelled yet.

A rule for it

The kitchen rules already asked for 1.0 m round every island. That passes an island no wheelchair can turn beside. The new hard rule, KZ-step-free, holds every step-free home's kitchen to two things. There is a seated work place: a worktop at least 0.8 m wide, at most 0.80 m high, with 0.70 m of knee room under it. And there are 1.5 m between an island's long sides and the worktop or tall unit each one faces. The numbers come from DIN 18040-2 and the ADA. Each can be tuned in the rules file, and tests plant a fault at both ends of each range. Last version's U kitchen fails it, because it had no seated place. The island passes.

What the planner had to learn

Twice the kitchen planner put the twin fridge where it shouldn't stand. First it went across the yard window, which broke a hard rule (no tall piece in front of a window). Then it went into the island's ring, which narrowed the north way. Neither had ever been checked when the planner placed a fridge. Now it keeps cold appliances off windows and out of an island's ring, and it carries no run into the ring. The fridge stays on the north wall, beside the corner cupboard.

The score

Overall the index stays at 73.6. The first-floor home goes from 85.2 to 85.4. Every hard rule that passed before still passes.
