38. A machine for every home

17 November 2026

You said on 17 November: "add a rule to have one washing machine in each unit, and also one dishwashing machine in each unit, proportional to their size: for the smaller units a smaller dishwashing machine and for the larger units larger ones. The same for the refrigerator and also the freezer: for larger units two side by side, and for smaller units a smaller freezer and refrigerator. Look at the standards in industry and make them a bit larger."

A change of mind about the dishes

The kitchen work of chapter 35 made washing up by hand the default, because fewer than one urban Iranian home in ten owns a dishwasher; the dishwasher was only an empty slot in C and D. Your rule replaces that: every home now has one. The drainer and the dish rack stay beside every sink, for the polo pot, the herb bowl and the samovar.

What the industry says, and one step more

  • Dishwashers come 45 cm wide (9-10 place settings) or 60 cm wide (12-14). The 60 cm is usual from four people; here it starts at three.
  • Fridges want about 100 litres for one person and 50 more for each further person; freezers 40-80 litres a person, and Iranian homes, which freeze bread, herbs and meat, sit at the top of that. The sizes on the market are a ladder: a 60 cm fridge-freezer (about 237 + 87 litres), a 70 cm one (330 + 108), and two full-height 60 cm columns side by side, a fridge and a freezer (346 + 242), the Iranian "twin". Each home gets the step that holds its need, and one more; from three people, the two columns.
  • Washing machines: 5-7 kg for one or two, 7-9 kg for three or four, 10 kg for five. Each home gets a kilo more than the middle: 8, 9 or 10 kg. They all stand in 60 x 60 cm. A "slim" washer is not narrower but shallower, about 47 cm deep; no home needs one.

The sizes follow the people who live in each home (A 3, B 4, C 3, D 2). All the numbers, and the sources, are in the rules AP-washer, AP-dishwasher and AP-cold, and you can change them.

Where they went

  • A (3): a 9 kg washer in the bath, a 60 cm dishwasher, and the two columns in the kitchen's corner, facing south. The old fridge there faced a worktop's end: its door could hardly open.
  • B (4): a 10 kg washer on the children's floor, the 60 cm dishwasher in the island, and the two columns on the party wall, below the concrete column.
  • D (2): an 8 kg washer by the balcony, a 45 cm dishwasher beside the sink, and a 70 cm fridge-freezer in the kitchen's north corner.
  • C, the caretaker's family (3): a 9 kg washer and a 60 cm dishwasher, but no wall for the two columns. The alcove beside the riser is 1.1 m, and no other wall leaves the doors room to open. That is a question for you, with the options drawn: the columns in the hall beside the table, with a larder in the alcove, or a 70 cm fridge-freezer with the hob's worktop 0.6 m shorter. See the card wip-ap-c-cold.

The planner looked for each place along every wall of the kitchen, never cutting a worktop below 1.8 m, keeping 0.6 m clear in front of the doors and every way through the kitchen open, and leaving no thin gap at either end. With the dishwasher, the weekly clean-up gets shorter: the kitchens' scenario scores fell in C (4,370 to 3,790) and D (3,850 to 3,500). They rose a little in A, and more in B (2,690 to 3,030), because B's columns stand further from the entry than the old fridge did.
