The story

63. The soft rules weighed

20 November 2026

The building had over a hundred soft rules, and the owner asked which of them matter: "please do this triage ... and then accordingly choose the new items to be optimized against and adapt the design please and update relevant files." The first half, the triage, sorted them into three: rules to make hard, rules to weigh more in daily life, and polish. It also found eleven rules that measured the wrong thing. This chapter is the second half.

Rules that measured wrongly

The drains' fall rule read the same floor depth everywhere and forgot that the upper bath of the big home sits on a recessed slab. Read right, five drains were still a few millimetres short, and the slab now steps down 60 mm under four rooms. The branch length rule now knows an air admittance valve: twelve long branches became three, all in that same upper bath. A riser's capacity was measured in the narrow part every floor shares, not in that floor's own box. The way rule had been blaming furniture for halls that are simply narrower than a way; those halls are now their own question about the plan. The door rule held two different faults, a leaf that stands out and a latch with no room for a hand, and now holds them apart. A rule that only suggests things to fill a gap no longer counts against the score. Four were left for later, each with its reason.

Hard rules

Twenty-seven rules are now hard: the stairs' rise, going, guards and handrails, the hob's window and shelves, bath doors that open out, the step-free bath, the fans and their ducts, no drain under a neighbour, the drains' fall, the risers' capacity and continuity, a storey's height and a room's glass. They all pass; a later change that breaks one now shows as a drawing error. A vent that came up in the roof's vine seat is carried up the pergola's post instead. Three tier-one rules stay soft, on purpose. The stair box and a stretcher lift both need the core's wall moved: two square metres of the big home, one each of the grandparents' and the caretaker's, two of the basement, and the big home's north hall would fall below the width the connection rule now demands. The numbers come from secondary sources. The engineer and the lift maker answer first.

Weighed up

The daily-life rules count double in the goals, and the searches' weights moved out of the code into the owner's rules file. Raised as the triage proposed, they moved nothing that is built: every home's plan came out the same. What still flags is made by the rooms themselves, the kitchens a little short of worktop, a few halls narrower than a way, not by where a piece was put. No floor changed in any home.
