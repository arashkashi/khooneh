The story

72. A clear way home

21 November 2026

The owner looked again at the big home's living floor, the fourth: "the connection from the kitchen in the 4th floor to the bedrooms go through the storage space, I mean this is a cool idea but I must say it would not be super acceptable by other people. we would like to have a clear connection and also have a nice storage without compromising on any other aspects we like, maybe try to experiment with the former air shaft space, and also re-positioning the small bathroom."

Two days earlier the walk-through pantry had become a hall, so that the front door reached the couple's room at all (the connection rule). But the hall was only a metre wide, the pantry's shelves stood open along one side behind a curtain, and the kitchen's lost storage had come back as four tall cupboards in the family hall. From the kitchen to the bedroom you walked 3.5 m along open shelves and past the cupboards. The rule asked that a way exists; it never asked what the way is like.

The owner's judgement as a rule

The new hard rule, MV-clear, takes every main way of every home: from each floor's front door to every bedroom, living room, kitchen and study, and from the kitchen to every bedroom. It walks each one the way the connection rule finds it, along the middle of a hall and straight across a wider room, round a riser's box rather than through it. Every hall on the way must be at least 1.0 m wide among the pieces standing in it (a narrowing shorter than 0.8 m, beside a column or at a door frame, counts as a doorway and needs 0.7 m). At most 30% of a way may run beside tall storage, a wardrobe, a cupboard or a tall shelf within 0.75 m of the walking line. And no part of it may run along an open store or a pantry alcove. All four numbers are in the owner's rules file, each tested with a planted fault at both ends of its range. The household essentials planner, which places tall cupboards where a home is short of storage, now asks the rule first and puts none where a way would go over its share.

What it found

B's top floor failed, as the owner said. So did the small duplex's upper entry on the third floor: its front door opens beside the laundry and linen store in the old shaft's strip, open behind a curtain along 1.7 m of the 4.3 m way to the bedroom. Every other home passed; the most lined way is the grandparents' from their door to their room, 9%, past one utility cupboard.

Because a failing hard rule caps the comfortable-homes goal for every home, both had to be fixed. The small duplex's store got a wall and a hinged door on the entry's side, its washing machine and shelves where they were (0.08 m² of D's usable floor to the wall).

Four layouts, built and measured

Each was written into a throwaway copy of the design and built by the whole pipeline, the plan author, the interiors, the kitchen, bath and essentials planners and every rule, then measured on the same footing.

  • C1, the least change. Wall the pantry off the way and enter it from the living room. The way stays 0.9 m clear, and the pantry, 0.85 m deep, holds a third of what the kitchen needs on its floor. Out.
  • C3, the widest hall. The WC into the old shaft's strip, the pantry into the WC's old place, the way widened to 1.85 m. The shortest way from the kitchen, but the pantry is too small for the kitchen's store and the coat bay goes. Out.
  • C2, the owner's first hint. The WC into the old shaft's strip off the entry, the WC's old place and the coat nook one long hall, the old way and the pantry one walk-in pantry. It passes everything; the coat and shoe bay goes.
  • K1, built. The WC moves the other way, to the old entry's street end beside the couple's bath. Its old place becomes a plain hall from the entry to the family side's door. The old way and the pantry become one walk-in pantry, 1.85 by 3.45 m, shelves on both long walls, its hinged door at the kitchen end of the living room. The coat bay in the old shaft's strip stays.

What K1 gives, and what it costs

From the front door to the couple's room is now 6.7 m, with nothing stored along it. The pantry and the kitchen hold 2,702 litres on their own floor, more than the 2,300 the big home keeps, so the four tall cupboards leave the family hall. The usable floor of the big home rises from 128.75 to 131.28 m², because the strip that was a hall is store again and the hall from the entry is the WC's old place. The first view from the front door is the same (three quarters living room), the sofa still gets in, the kitchen, the store under the stair, the coat bay and the family side's hinged door stay, and no window moves.

Three things are traded, and the owner should know them. The way from the kitchen to the bedroom is longer, 13.2 m through the entry instead of 9.2 m through the pantry. The WC is smaller, 3.0 m² instead of 3.7, still a WC and a basin. And its drain runs 3.3 m to the riser, so the slab steps down 60 mm under it, as it already does under the grandparents' bath and WC; the engineer confirms the recess. The walk-in pantry keeps a hinged door rather than a curtain, so guests at the table see a door and not shelves; the small-spaces rule flags that, softly. Each choice is on its own card, open for the owner's review.
