The story

39. Where the pipes go

17 November 2026

The owner asked us to "review the positions of the risers ... see if you could minimise the number of risers, or reposition them better. We need to define what would be a better position for the risers." The same day they asked what the empty box at the bottom left of A's store was. It is riser R2, drawn as a room with no name.

What "better" means

A riser is now measured on two scales. The first is the service it gives:

  • it stands in one place from the ground floor to the roof;
  • it holds everything it carries on its busiest floor;
  • every drain reaches it along its own home's floor, within the drain's length and the fall the floor allows;
  • nothing crosses another home;
  • every bath fan and kitchen hood has a short duct;
  • its vent comes out on the roof away from the beds and seats.

The second is what it costs the design: the floor it takes (dearer where people sit or sleep), the notches it leaves, where its hatch opens, a soil stack beside a bedroom, an opening by a shear wall, and how many risers there are. These are seven rules (RS-*), with every weight in the rules file.

What we found

R3, the riser by the landing, is 0.3 m deep. It cannot hold what it carries on any floor: a lagged soil pipe with its vent, the greywater pipe and two bath ducts need 0.35 m behind a removable face. So the engineer's question about D's guest WC is answered: not at 0.3 m.

A search then tried one to four risers against the party walls, the columns, the core, today's boxes and the corners of the wet rooms:

  • One riser, or two, or a single central spine, leaves the west baths or B's upstairs bath without a drain.
  • Four risers put a box in three living rooms and a bedroom.
  • Three is the right number, in today's places.

The proposal

The recommendation is to deepen R3 by 5 cm, from 0.30 to 0.35 m, and to pull R2 back off the column it runs through on every floor, which the structure study found. R2 holds just as much without the column inside it. The score falls from 562 to 482.

A slimmer R2 is your choice. R2 needs about 0.8 × 0.7 m, so its north face can move 0.4 m. That gives 0.45 m² back on every floor, to A's store and to the caretaker's fridge alcove. The cost: every drain of the west baths grows 0.4 m.

A fourth stack is also on the card, proposed by the study of A's bath. It would stand in the notch beside the column in the west baths, and it brings every drain of A's and the caretaker's baths within reach. But the soil pipe would run beside four bedrooms, and B gets a box in the corner of its hall. It scores within about 1 % of the recommendation, so it is a real choice for you, not a clear win.

Every riser and shaft on every plan, sheet and decision page is now hatched and named, with what it carries. The services engineer signs off the sizes and falls.
