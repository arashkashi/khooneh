The story

77. Nothing empty, nothing black

21 November 2026

The owner looked at the grandparents' bathroom on the design page and saw two things. There was a strange empty space beside the stand-up shower. And there were black shapes they could not name. They asked for all of it to be fixed, and for rules so it could not happen again.

What the black shapes were

Three different things were drawn black, and each one had its own cause.

  • The ceiling airer and the shelf over the washing machine. Both were drawn the right way, as dashed outlines above the plan's cut. But each one also carries an invisible band, so that pointing at it shows its name and height. The floor sheets style that band. The design page had its own hand-made copy of the sheets' style rules, and the copy had lost that one line. A browser paints an unstyled shape black. So the design page drew every piece mounted overhead as a black block: 31 pieces across the building (10 towel-hook rails, 7 shower shelves, 6 coat rails, 4 airers and 4 washer shelves).
  • The tall block on the west wall. This is the concrete column, 0.5 × 1.2 m. It was drawn correctly: structure is drawn solid on a plan. But nothing said what it was. Every column and concrete wall now gives its name and size when you point at it, on the design page and on the floor sheets.
  • "washer_shelf". When the page's list of short names had no entry for a piece, the label fell back to the code's internal name. Nineteen kinds of piece had no short name. Labels now come from the catalogue's own names, in English and Persian, and never from the code's names.

The design page and the decision pages now take their style rules from the same single source as the floor sheets, so they cannot drift apart again.

What the empty space was

On the published page, the level shower stood 0.5 m off the west wall. That left a pocket 0.5 × 0.85 m between the shower, the two walls and the column. Nobody could use it or clean it. The same pocket, in the same room shape two floors up, was the caretaker's dead floor on 11 November. The rules written then only warned about it, so the layout search kept choosing it.

The page the owner saw was already older than the design. Earlier today, a change to give the bath a full-size basin had re-arranged the room. The pocket was gone, but something worse had taken its place. The basin stood in the south-west corner, and the washing machine and the WC boxed in the floor in front of it, leaving only a 0.2 m slot. No one could reach the basin to wash.

The bath, laid out again

The room is 2.6 × 2.9 m. It holds a tub, a level shower, a WC with room for a wheelchair in front of it, a basin and the washing machine, and the 1.5 m turning circle the grandparents' home keeps. The column takes a bite out of the west wall. The room optimiser searched it again with the new rule. Moving the fixtures alone, no arrangement passed every rule: the best one left 0.6 m in front of the WC, where 1.1 m is needed. Its next tier may move a light wall. The best answer moves the wall between the bath and A's store 0.2 m south. The bath gains 0.53 m², the store loses the same, and A's home keeps exactly the floor it had:

  • a 1.7 m tub runs along the north wall, over the corner pocket the owner saw empty;
  • the washing machine stands against the column's face, away from the water of the tub and the shower;
  • the 0.6 m basin stands in the south-west corner, and the floor in front of it now opens to the room;
  • the WC sits on the south wall, with its grab-rail side and 1.1 m clear in front;
  • the level shower takes the south-east corner.

One other arrangement moved no wall: the WC in the corner pocket, with the door where the door search already slides it. But its basin, in the far corner, would drain 5.4 m to the riser, deeper than the floor allows. The architect chose the wall move, as the owner has asked, and it stays open for the owner's review.

Two rules, so it does not happen again

  • DS-wet (must hold). In a bath or WC, the layout may not leave a dead patch of 0.1 m² or more: a strip too narrow to clean, or a pocket nobody reaches. And no fixture may have the floor in front of it boxed in. The owner can set the size between 0.06 and 0.2 m². The bath search now counts a breach as a fault, not just a cost. The rule also measures each bath with its door where the door search actually puts it.
  • PD-complete (must hold). Every piece drawn on a plan has a real plan symbol. It has a name in English and Persian. If it hangs overhead, it is drawn dashed above the cut. And no page leaves a shape unstyled. The design page will not even be written if it would break this rule.
