The story

51. The free middle

18 November 2026

Level −1 is the residents' floor: a small gym on the street side, an open middle, and the shared room round the glass garden pocket and the birch. You asked for two things. First: "Move the toilet and also the tea area to one of the corners. I want the middle space to be free for future experimentation", and then, "maybe the tea corner in the bottom left and the toilet in bottom right attached to the air shaft". Second, the ceiling of −1 as high as the rules allow, since it may become a gym.

Where things were

The wet room stood against the west wall beside the main drain, with a narrow pipe box beside it, and the tea corner filled the same wall further south. Between them and the gym, the middle was cut into three strips of floor, none of them free.

The tea corner, bottom left

The tea corner now takes the south-west corner of the shared room, by the glass to the court. Its counter, 1.8 m long, stands under the glass: the glass starts at 0.9 m, the height of the worktop, so you make tea looking out at the stone basin and the birch, and the glass and the view stay as they were. A small fridge sits under the counter, and shelves behind a curtain stand on the party wall beside it. The sink cannot drain by gravity, because −1 is below the street sewer; a small pump under the counter lifts its water to the drain in the ceiling.

The toilet, against the old shaft

The very south-east corner is the way out to the court: the door to the court stair is there. A wet room of 1.5 m in that corner would leave 0.80 m beside the column, less than the 0.9 m door, and there is no other place for that door over the stair's landing. So the wet room goes where your second message puts it: against the air shaft, the closed box beside the lift, on the east wall just south of it. It is the same size inside as before, 1.45 by 2.0 m, with a WC, a small basin and a level shower. Its door is in its west wall, beside the column, clear of the foyer's opening and of the way and the view from the lift to the court.

The toilet has no window, and your idea gives its fan a way out: the duct goes 0.3 m through the shaft's wall and up the box to the roof garden. The box was closed so that smoke and fire on one floor cannot reach the homes beside it, so the duct is a fire-rated one with a fire damper where it leaves the toilet, and every floor of the box stays sealed round it. A new rule checks this: a duct in a closed box must keep the box closed. Its drains go to a small pumping station in a sealed pit beside it.

The free middle

The pipe box is gone, and the whole west wall and the middle are one open floor of about 35 m² from the gym to the shared room. Only the main drain's riser stays in the north-west, where it has always been. The games cupboard moves into the small corner south of the wet room, and the folding game table to the shared room's west half.

A higher −1

You also asked for the −1 ceiling to be as high as the rules allow, since it may become a gym. The national building code sets only least heights (2.40 m for rooms people use), not a greatest. Our ground floor is at the street's level, so −1 stays a basement however deep it goes. The court still lights it as the code asks: at least 20 m², 4.5 m wide, open to the sky. The number that limits it is the storey limit that the fourth floor already uses: 3.60 m from floor to floor. No figure was found for Qazvin; that limit is Tehran's, on streets wider than 8 m.

So −1 is now 3.60 m from floor to floor, not 3.12. Under the slab it has 3.20 m clear, against 2.72 before. The gym has no beam over it, so it gets the full height a gym wants. Under the beams at its south edge and across the shared room it has 2.90 m. Nothing above the ground floor moves: −1 and −2 go 0.48 m deeper, to −3.60 and −6.72. That means about 100 m³ more digging, basement walls holding back 6.72 m of earth instead of 6.24 m, and a deep pit that was already in the code's highest class. The court goes down with −1, so its stair becomes 20 steps of 18 cm with a longer half landing, and the lift travels half a metre more. Raising the ground floor instead would have lifted the whole building and its roof.

One question is for the permit office. Some cities cap a basement at 2.20 to 2.60 m clear, so that it cannot become a home. The old 2.72 m already passed that, so the question was there before. If Qazvin says no, −1 goes back to 3.12 m, and that is a change of two numbers. A new rule, which you can tune, checks every storey against the limit. research/minus1-height.md has the sources and the sums.
