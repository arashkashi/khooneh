87. The plant room's way up

22 November 2026

The owner, looking at the −2 sheet: "there is no riser here? is it correct?"

Yes, by design, but nothing showed it

No riser stands in the plant room. The boiler's flue runs along the ceilings of the plant room and the stores, comes up through the slab into a fire-rated casing on −1, and runs along the −1 ceilings into R1, the supply riser. The heating pipes, the pumped cold water and the rain tank's lines also leave along the ceilings to a riser's foot. The services file said all this in words. No drawing showed it, and the casing on −1 was on no plan and in no 3D model. A reader had to conclude the plant room was cut off.

Drawn from the data

Each route is now planned as data: a line along the ceiling on each level, at the ceiling's height under the beams, joined where it goes through the slab. A route never runs over the stair, the lift, a landing or a home. The −2 and −1 sheets draw the routes as thin dashed lines: brown for the flue (مسیر دودکش زیر سقف) and blue for the pipes (لوله‌های موتورخانه تا رایزر), with a ring where each goes through the slab. The flue's casing is now a room of its own size, 0.35 m square, on the plan and in the 3D model. It takes 0.12 m² of the foyer's floor.

The boiler had no place in the data either. It now hangs on the plant room's wall by the stores, beside the door. The planned flue is 4.85 m on −2 and 2.70 m on −1, 7.55 m in all with six bends, under the 8 m limit. The services said 7.15 m.

What drawing it showed

The services put the casing in the foyer's "south-west corner". That corner is over earth, because −2 stops 1.8 m short of it. Only the foyer's north band lies over the floor below, a 0.3 m strip between R3 and the landing's door. The casing fits there only by standing 0.10 m into that door's opening, which cuts the door's clear width from 0.72 m to 0.62 m, under the 0.7 m minimum.

The pipes go up through R3's foot, the only other way from −2 to −1. Its opening through the slab is 0.20 × 0.50 m clear. That cannot hold the eight heating pipes (a flow and a return per home) and the cold main.

Two new hard rules make this a check and not a matter of reading:

  • RS-plant-route. Everything the plant room sends up has an unbroken route to its riser. A pipe run is at most 15 m long, a figure the owner can set from 8 to 25 m.
  • RS-casing. Every casing or riser a route goes up through is on the plan. A casing holds what goes up in it, stands over real floor and stands clear of doors.

Both faults are now listed for the owner on card wip-plant-way-up.

Would a straight shaft be better?

It sounds simpler: a flue straight up from the plant room, with no 7.55 m of horizontal run. But that shaft would pass through the gym, the lobby, A's and C's second bedrooms, B's reading corner and B's couple's bath. That is 0.12 m² from A, 0.12 m² from C and 0.25 m² from B, and an inspection hatch where a flue passes through a bedroom. It would also do nothing for the pipes. So it is not better.

The card offers three choices:

  • Widen R3's foot below the ground floor to about 0.85 × 0.7 m. This costs about 0.7 m² of shared floor below ground and no floor in any home. It is recommended.
  • The straight shaft.
  • Put the boiler on the stair-house roof. It would need no flue indoors, but the gas company and the engineer must accept it.

The engineer checks whether 7.7 m with six bends suits the boiler's flue.
