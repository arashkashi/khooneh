52. The door that faces the hall

18 November 2026

You passed on some feedback: "the main entrance to a unit should face the living room or kitchen or common spaces, and not face the toilet and bathroom and bedrooms." You asked for it to be checked, and made a rule if it holds.

Is it right?

Yes. It is the Persian house's hashti: you step in, turn, and walk to the talar, and the family's rooms are never in the first view. It also matches finding 8 of the architect's review: in three homes the front door opened at the bedroom end. But a rule that forbids any bedroom door in an entry hall would be too strict. In a compact flat the hall serves the bedrooms, and a bedroom door on the side wall, out of the first view, is fine. A guest WC beside the entry is good practice: guests should not walk to the family's bath. And the guest room by the door, the biruni room, belongs there. What matters is what the door faces, and what you see in your first steps.

The rule

The new rule, P-entry-view, stands people just inside each home's main front door, 1.0 and 1.5 m in, across the door's width, at eye height. They look along the way in, 30° to each side and 5 m deep. Walls, shut doors, tall furniture and the hashti's walnut lattice stop the eye. A shut bedroom or bath door counts as that room. At most a quarter of the view may land on bedrooms and baths, and a guest WC counts half. No bedroom, bath or WC door may stand straight ahead (within 12°) within 5 m. A door seen edge-on in a side wall is not "ahead". The rule is soft, scored under Privacy, and each of its numbers can be tuned in the rules file.

Every home, measured

The caretaker's home and the big family's top floor face their lattice screens, built for exactly this a few hours earlier. The small duplex faces its living room. The big family's children's floor has a second door, and from it a bedroom door shows at the side, 8% of the view. D's upper door sees only its little entry. The shared rooms at −1 face the court garden. One home failed: the first-floor home, the step-free one. Half of its first view was the bath's door and the couple room's, and the bath's door stood straight ahead, 3.7 m across the family hall.

Why not a screen in A

The lattice was left out of A on purpose: there it would narrow the wheelchair's way beside the bath door. The other small moves don't help either. Swapping a hinge doesn't change what a shut door shows. The bath door can't slide along its wall, because the tub stands on the rest of it.

The door moves instead

So the front door itself moved, 1.95 m south along the landing's west wall. That wall is a light partition, not structure. Now the door opens onto the family hall and the bath's plain wall. Only 2% of the first view is private, and no private door is ahead. The door now stands across the landing from the lift's door, so a wheelchair rolls straight from the lift into the home, with no turn on the 1.35 m landing. The guest room and the WC stay by the entry, and the walk from the door to the kitchen got shorter. This is an AI decision, open for your review (card wip-a-entry-door).

One thing broke on the way, and it was useful. The door search got stuck: the couple room's door and the bath's door clashed in every single move. The search now tries two clashing doors together, and it finds the old good pair again. The index is back to 73.6, and the first-floor home now scores 85.2.
