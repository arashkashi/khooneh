Chapter 10 · 6 November 2026

Moving the columns

The owner said it plainly: “I sense that you follow the plan of the pillars of year 1399.” It was true. The AI's draft had its inner column line at x 7.35, close to 1399's 7.43, and nobody had decided that. It had come along with the rest. That same line was why the cars didn't fit.

The owner also asked to model the 1403 design at last. Read from the sheets' own drawing data, it held a surprise: no column inside the building at all. Seven stand on the two side walls, with one short wall by the stair. It answers the owner's question from 2024 about why the slabs were waffles. They have to span nearly 10 m.

Decision. Column lines are a design variable. Each design chooses them, with a written reason, and an engineer confirms them.

Freedom has a price, so the AI compared six layouts. With no inner line the parking opens to 9.3 m wide and floors can differ freely. But by a rough rule the floor gets about 0.2 m deeper, which costs about 1.1 m of building height, or takes the ceilings below the 2.74 m the owner asked for. Almost all the stiffness across the plot would rest on one short wall near the street. The layout to test first is two lines about 1 m in from the side walls. It's a suggestion for the engineer, and nothing has been redrawn.

1403 taught other things. Its second floor needs no landing, so the building could still work if the fourth floor is refused. Its kitchens get closer to daylight than the draft's do. Its pipes and its 44 m² of private stairs are warnings. And one correction came from it: area targets taken from the sheets include walls, while the model measures rooms, so some earlier “misses” may be false.

While this was running, the owner asked for square metres on the maps and for the stairs between floors to be shown. Drawing them exposed two private stairs in 1399 that the data had lost. The owner also asked for a common data structure to show how the homes connect, and that work began alongside.
