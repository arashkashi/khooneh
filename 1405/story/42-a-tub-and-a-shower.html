42. A tub and a shower

17 November 2026

Looking at A's master bath, you saw "a lot of strange spaces" and asked for a stand-up shower as well as a bath tub, with the corners properly used. You also set the strategy: first leave the walls alone; then move them a little, or even the riser; the columns last, and only if the building stays sound. "Give enough space to find a good solution but not so much space as to confuse yourself." Later the same day you asked the same of D, the small duplex: it has no tub, but seems to have room for one.

The target first

Before any search, the numbers: a tub of at least 1.6 m; a separate shower of at least 0.9 m that is level, because A is the step-free home; the WC with 1.1 m in front; a basin; the washing machine 0.6 m from the water; a 1.5 m turning circle; no more than 4 % dead floor; and every drain within reach of a riser.

Four tiers, each a fence

A new tool, the room optimiser, searches a room in tiers and opens the next only when the one before cannot reach the target. Tier 0 moves the fixtures only: every tub length, both level showers, both basins, along every wall and column face, 2,015 arrangements. Tier 1 moves the light walls by up to 0.4 m and the door, and re-checks every neighbour that gives up floor. Tier 2 moves or adds a riser, checked on every floor it passes. Tier 3 would screen a column move for the engineer; it was never needed.

What came out for A

Tier 0 already fits everything: a 1.6 m tub along the east wall, a level walk-in shower in the north-west corner, the WC tucked into the notch beside the column, a small basin and the washer, with every wet-room check passing. It is in the design now. What it costs: the walk-in shower shrinks from 1.2 to 0.9 m, enough for a folding seat but not for a helper; and the drains still run too far to the riser behind the store. Moving A's hall wall 0.1 m gives a 1.7 m tub. Only a riser cures the drains: a new stack in the dead notch beside the column brings the drains of A's bath, and of the caretaker's bath above it, within 3 m of it, all but two by the riser study's stricter measure (A's washer and C's shower, about 3.3-3.5 m). It costs a soil pipe beside four bedrooms and a small box in B's halls, so it is your decision, on the card together with the riser study's own option.

What came out for D

D's shower room is 3.7 m². No arrangement of a tub beside a separate shower, the WC and the basin fits in it, and moving its walls by up to 0.4 m into the bedroom and the wardrobe alcove still leaves either the tub or the WC out. The smallest bath in the building with a tub and a separate shower, B's on the fourth floor, has 5.6 m². So D keeps its shower room, unless you want to give it about a square metre more.
