The story

89. The shared ways

22 November 2026

The owner asked the architect to settle every open card "to the best of your knowledge and objectively correct". This chapter covers four cards about the parts everyone shares: the street door, the ground-floor walkway, the landings, and −2's edge behind the lift. One of them changes the plan. The other three change finishes and lights, and they do not change the floor.

−2's edge: the sliver goes

The floor audit of 19 November found 0.67 m² at −2 that belonged to nothing. It was a strip 0.15 to 0.25 m wide between the core's south wall and the retaining wall, from the landing to the east party wall, and no one could reach it. Option A had already been chosen but not built. It is built now.

The landing runs south to the retaining wall, so it is 1.35 × 4.2 m instead of 1.35 × 3.9 m. Behind the lift there was a 0.15 m gap between the lift's south shear wall and the retaining wall, and nothing can use 0.15 m. There the retaining wall is carried on to the party wall and cast against the lift wall, so −2 ends at the lift wall's face and no gap is left between the two walls.

The floor audit, run before and after, measures the result:

  • The landing gains 0.42 m².
  • The gap goes from 0.67 m² to nothing.
  • The footprint is 0.34 m² smaller, because that part is now concrete.

The audit's known fault on −2 is cleared for real, and the seismic estimate does not change. The structural engineer confirms the wall behind the lift and its thickness.

The walkway beside the cars

The nearest car bay is 0.10 m from the lobby's walkway, and nothing marks the edge. The obvious answer is a barrier, and it is wrong. The cars drive in nose first. The driver sits on the left, so the driver's door of each car beside the walkway opens towards it. A 1.8 m car centred in its 2.3 m bay leaves 0.35 m beside its body, and getting out takes about 0.9 m. The walkway is the drivers' door space too.

Bollards or a planter would trap the driver in the car. Moving the cars west to make room would take the 0.2 m between the two cars and the west aisle, which the bicycles and the meter cabinet already use.

So the walkway gets its own slip-resistant floor from the street door to the yard. A 0.1 m painted line runs along its edge, with a contrasting strip in front of the lift and the stair. A child can see where the way is, and the drivers' doors still open.

The landings

Every landing is a 1.35 × 3.9 m corridor with no daylight. All seven landings get:

  • a warm wall light beside the lift;
  • a light over each home's front door, six in all;
  • the floor's number beside the lift, raised so it can also be read by touch.

The wall facing the lift gets a finish on the four home floors and on −1, where people arrive. On −2, which holds only the plant room and the stores, and at the roof door, washable paint is enough.

One change from the card: the finish is tile, stone, or a timber veneer rated for an escape route, not bare timber, because the landing is the stair's way out. The finish is 10 to 15 mm thick and the lights hang above head height, so the landing keeps its width.

The street door

From the street the 1.2 m door and the car opening read as one dark gap. The door gets:

  • a slim steel-and-glass canopy, 1.6 m wide and 0.8 m deep, about 2.5 m up;
  • a warm lamp on a dusk sensor;
  • the house number, lit by the lamp.

The canopy keeps the rain off whoever waits at the door with the shopping. A brick portal would not, and it would also step out over the street line.

The card's stone plinth for parcels is dropped. The facade stands on the street line, so the plinth would sit on the public pavement. Parcels get a shelf at bench height inside, beside the mailboxes and clear of the door's swing. It folds down. The walkway there is 1.45 m between the cars' edge and the core wall, and a fixed 0.3 m shelf would leave 1.15 m, less than the 1.2 m needed to push a motorcycle through. Folded, the shelf leaves 1.40 m.

The canopy reaches 0.8 m over the street line, so it waits on the building permit. If the municipality says no, the lamp and the number are built without it.
