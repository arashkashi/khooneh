37. A bath on the terrace

17 November 2026

You asked: "Could you please move the master bathroom to the 7.8 m² terrace space on the 4th floor? I guess the one terrace for the large duplex on the third floor is good enough. Now that this could be moved, maybe we could use this space in a creative manner."

What the terrace can hold

The terrace beside the couple's room is 2.7 × 2.9 m inside, walled to 1.7 m and open to the sky. Enclosed, it holds a tub, a separate shower, a WC and two basins, 8.6 m² against today's 5.6 m². Your 1.5 m corner tub fits there too, beside a separate shower, which it never did in the old bath. The door goes where the terrace door was, in the bedroom's east wall at the street end. It opens out into the bedroom, as every bath door does, and rests on the street wall, so the bedroom keeps its layout. The night walk from the bed to the WC drops from 7.9 m to 3.2 m.

What it costs

  • The permit. A roofed, walled room counts as floor area. An open terrace does not, or is charged apart. This adds about 9.6 m², and the top floor's plate becomes 137 m², the same as the floors below. Ground cover and height don't change, and no rule made this terrace: it was a design choice from iteration 2. Because nothing is built yet, the bath goes on the permit drawings from the start. Enclosing a terrace after the permit is the kind of extra floor area the Article 100 commission fines, or orders taken down. The question is Qazvin's density allowance for this plot, which the project hasn't found. It is for the permit office.
  • The drains. The nearest riser, R3, is 3.6 m away. A pipe in the top floor's own floor would cross the entry hall 225 mm high. In the ceiling of the floor below, it passes only B's own rooms: the reading corner, the play hall and the children's bath. That run is 3.75 m at 1:50, in a 225 mm box with 2.50 m clear under it. The tub, the shower, the basins and the floor drain then run 4.9–6.6 m to the riser, against the 3 m of an unvented branch, so they need vented branches. A riser of its own on the street would have to pass through the caretaker's and A's rooms below, so it was rejected. The engineer confirms both, and whether R3's narrow 0.3 m stretches take a WC's soil pipe.
  • The slab. The bath's floor is recessed about 160 mm, for the WC's soil pipe and the falls. This is cheap now, because nothing is cast. A raised floor would do instead, but it puts a single odd step at the bedroom door.
  • Light and privacy. The street window lines up with the windows below it, frosted to 1.8 m. The bedroom keeps its two street windows and loses the glazed terrace door. B's outdoor space falls from 6.1 to 4.6 m² a person.

The freed room

Each idea was scored against what the tools measure.

  • A dressing room isn't next to the bedroom.
  • A study there would have no window.
  • B doesn't need another bath or a bigger WC.
  • A laundry would break your rule that the washing machine lives in a bath.

The pantry wins. B had no store of its own: its rice, bottled water, tomato paste and torshi, eleven things and 737 litres, lived in the shared store five floors down. The old bath becomes a walk-through pantry. Shelves run down both sides, with one door to the family hall and one to the living room by the kitchen.

Yours to decide

Four options are drawn and measured:

  • keep the terrace;
  • the bath with one door, from the bedroom (recommended, built on branch bbath);
  • the same bath with a second door from the entry hall;
  • a smaller shower room behind a 1.3 m street loggia, without the tub.

The decision page is wip-b-bath-terrace.
