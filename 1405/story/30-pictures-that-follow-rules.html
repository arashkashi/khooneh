The story

30. Pictures that follow rules

10 November 2026

On 9 November you asked us to look again at every picture of design 1405 and to improve them: more detail, the light corrected, and rules for where the camera stands. Two reviews looked at all 207 key pictures of the walks and all 64 snapshots, one at a time.

What the reviews found

About one walk picture in four was lost to a wall, a pier, a door leaf or a wardrobe in front of the room. The old rule only measured the middle distance of the view, so a pier filling half the frame in front of a deep room passed. Most room snapshots were frames lifted from the films: tilted, often taken from a doorway, and 17 of 39 looked out of the room they were named after. The homes were built but not lived in: no pictures on the walls, the same bowl of fruit on every table, no towels, no toys.

What changed

The camera now follows rules you can tune, like the design rules: no occluder in front of the view, no flat wall filling the frame, a deep enough key picture, no standing in a doorway, a level eye, no two pictures of one view, and a closing view that looks into the home. The rules were set on the reviewers' own judgements of the 207 pictures. The room snapshots are now composed shots: a level camera that keeps walls upright, standing inside the room and looking along it or across from a corner.

What is left for you

Some things the pictures show are the design itself, not the camera, so they became questions on the decision pages rather than silent changes: B's two narrow children's rooms, C's bedroom windows, where D's washer stands, B's walled terrace, halls with four doors in view, the long blank wall at −1, the dining table that leads the living rooms, and whether a walk may end at the home's best view instead of back at its door.
