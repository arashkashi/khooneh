The story

56. A lived-in home

19 November 2026

The rooms had their beds, sofas, tables and wardrobes, and little else. You asked for "a bit more furniture ... plants etc., for the living room book shelves ... not much", and that the success should be measured, not guessed. So one planner now goes through every room and adds what makes a home look lived in: a rug under the sofa, bookcases on blank walls, plants where a window gives them enough light, and a reading lamp beside the reading chair where it fits.

Measured, not guessed

Each piece has a number behind it. Bookcases come in 0.8 m modules, about 0.4 m of shelf for each person in the home. A plant stands only where a window gives it enough daylight. Nothing is placed on a way through a room, in front of a door, under a window's radiator or in the sweep of a casement. Every piece keeps the ways as they were. In the step-free home on the first floor, the wheelchair's 1.5 m turning circle stays. And no room becomes more than 40% furniture, so the rooms do not fill up. A piece that breaks any of this is not placed. The plans, the design page and the 3D model all show the same pieces now; the 3D model no longer adds rugs and plants of its own.

The children's homework corner

On the big home's children's floor there was a sitting corner by the street window. You were not sure what would happen there. It is now the children's homework corner: a desk for two along the window, its top under the sill and 5 cm off the wall for the radiator, a reading armchair with a lamp where the sofa stood, a bookcase on the side wall and a plant. A parent passing the play hall sees them at work.

You then agreed ("okay I agree with you") that the two children's bedrooms stay equal and the corner stays where it is, with the stair wall as its buffer. It is a shared study now. Later it can become the youngest's own room: a short wall and a hinged door added in its opening to the play hall. The desk's west metre stays, and a single bed fits beside it. Two things to know: a hinged wardrobe does not also fit, so clothes go in a wardrobe in the play hall or under a cabin bed; and at 6.2 m² and 2.05 m wide it would be smaller than a single bedroom should be (7.5 m² and 2.15 m).

The small duplex, tried

The small duplex's living room was left out at first, while its table was still being decided. Now that it has its table for four, the planner tried it like the other homes, and nothing fits: no blank wall a bookcase long with floor in front of it, no rug that keeps clear of the walls, tall pieces and door swings, no free spot near a window for a plant, no floor beside the seat for a lamp. The room is small and its ways are already tight. So it stays as it is, and the checks for books and plants say so for the small duplex rather than being switched off.

The planner's choices are AI decisions, open for your review. The homework corner is the AI's proposal; its future as a room is your decision.
