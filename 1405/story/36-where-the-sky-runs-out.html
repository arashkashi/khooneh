36. Where the sky runs out

17 November 2026

Reading the facade table, you asked: "There are parts where the percentage of sky is almost zero; do you have suggestions for this?" Your screenshot showed a second-floor window "on the side, facing the terrace", 6.0 m² of glass that sees no sky and no sun on any day, and yard rows at 4 % and 6 %.

First, which design it was

Those rows were iteration 2's, not 1405's. The daylight page shows several designs and remembered the one picked on an earlier visit, and nothing on the table said which design it showed. In iteration 2 that side window looked from the living room across a covered terrace, 2.25 m wide, at the side neighbour's wall, with the floor above over it: a window with no sky at all. 1405 has no such window. The page now always opens on the current design, names the design above the table, and puts a banner on any older one.

What takes the sky in 1405

A new measure follows every line from a window's centre to the sky. Where one is stopped, it charges the loss to the first thing it meets: the building behind, the side neighbour, the row across the street, our own balconies, floors or walls, or the earth around the court.

  • The street side (10.6 % on the first floor, 16.3 % on the second): all of it is the row across the 8 m alley. Nothing of ours stands in the way.
  • The yard side, first and second floors (5.3 % and 9.1 %): the building our size behind takes about 30 points, and our balconies 0.7 to 3.4. With that building 12 m away instead of 6 m, the same windows see 13.1 % and 19.7 %.
  • The third floor's yard (9.0 %): D's bedroom has two windows. The west one sees 13.1 %. The east one sees 3.2 %, because B's balcony above takes 16.6 points of its sky. This is the one that is ours to fix.
  • Below ground, the glass sees almost nothing: four floors stand over it. That was settled earlier, with electric light.

No window in 1405 turned out to be a modelling error: none sits inside a wall, faces back into the building or has something pressed against its glass.

Twelve fixes, measured

Each fix was measured on the rooms it touches: sky, daylight in the room, sun hours, summer heat and the winter balance. Raising the heads to the ceiling buys almost nothing. Paler reveals, ceilings and floors raise the daylight of every low room by about a sixth, which on the lower floors is more than any change to the sky can give. For D's bedroom, three changes work: one window instead of two, clear of the balcony; B's balcony moved over B's own loggia; or a grating floor that lets half the sky through.

A rule, and your choice

A new rule, WIN-sky-min, checks every window of a main room, not only the room's best one. It now flags D's east window. The options and the AI's recommendation are on wip-sky. The full numbers are in design/research/sky-1405.md.
