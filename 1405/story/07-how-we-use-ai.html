How we use artificial intelligence to design

This project began with a request for 3D models and high-quality renders. It changed direction within a day. The owner saw that pretty pictures would come too early, and asked for a design made of numbers that could be checked at every step. That has shaped how the AI is used ever since.

What the AI does

  • Reads and turns drawings into data. Every room in a design is a set of numbers in metres, so a computer can measure and test it.
  • Checks the design. It tests areas, overlaps, windows, pipes, parking, routes to every room and, now, sun on every floor.
  • Researches. Separate agents looked into Iran's housing market, digging a second basement, and windcatchers in Qazvin, marking what is measured, what is regulation and what is only inference.
  • Asks. It turns open choices into a form in Persian and English, with drawings and a stacking model to play with.
  • Proposes. It answered its own form and drew a design from those answers, so its guesses can be set against the owner's answers.
  • Criticises. A second agent, given only the design and not the reasoning, tried to break it.
  • Keeps this story.

What people do

The owner decides what matters. The early options were set aside because the owner said ideas should form through conversation, not branch too soon. The owner corrected the reading of 1399: the void beside a home was its high ceiling, and two labelled units were one home for a big family. The idea of moving the small unit to the street side came from the owner looking at a diagram. So did the real plots, and the two architects whose thinking now guides the feel of the building.

What surprised us. The AI was most confidently wrong when its own tests passed. Its first design passed all 36 tests, and five cars still didn't fit. After the redraw, a review found a court stair nobody could reach. Numbers from an old draft survived in the text. It misread one letter code as “bathrooms” in a report to the owner. Each mistake was found by checking the data again or by asking someone else to look, and most now have a test so they can't return.

Rules that came out of this

  • Every number should be traceable, or marked as a guess.
  • A passing test only proves what it tests.
  • The AI's answers stay hidden until people have given their own, so they don't steer anyone.
  • Nothing is downloaded without asking, and nothing private is published.

What the AI can't do

It can't visit the plot, test the ground or sign for the structure, and it doesn't know the family's life. Its regulation readings come from third-party copies. An engineer, an architect, the municipality and local agents still have to confirm what matters. The AI makes a first version and many checks cheap. It doesn't take responsibility for the building.
