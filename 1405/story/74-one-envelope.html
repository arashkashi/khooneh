The story

74. One envelope

21 November 2026

A house that serves several generations has to be cheap to keep warm for decades. Until today nothing in the project said how 1405's walls, roof and floors were insulated. The window studies assumed one figure for the wall, the shade study another for the roof, and an old study counted the windows at 2.8 where the drawings specify glass that gives 1.7. The fourth round of the roadmap for generations puts all of it in one place: a single file describing the building's skin, read by every calculation that counts heat. The gaps study had planned the same file under the same name, so there is one, not two.

The same numbers first

The first step only moved the numbers. Every study took its figures from the new file, unchanged, and every result came out exactly as before. A test now fails if any of them writes a U-value into its own code again.

Then one honest value for each part

The owner had handed these decisions to the architect. The architect put the insulation outside:

  • 100 mm of stone wool on the walls, outside the block and behind the brick, which stays as a ventilated skin. Stone wool does not burn.
  • 120 mm of insulation on the roof, under the pavers.
  • Breaks in the concrete where A's, D's and B's balconies pass through the wall, so the cold no longer runs into the rooms along them.
  • A target for airtightness, tested before the walls are finished.

The grandparents' floor over the open car park needed nothing new. The corner review had already put 100 mm of stone wool on the car park's ceiling for the floor heating. It is now counted once, from the same record, and gives 0.25. Adding another layer would have lowered the car park's ceiling below what cars need. That floor was one of A's largest losses. It now loses less than half as much.

Each part's value is now worked out from its layers. Two values went up because they are honest now. The walls to the unheated stair are 200 mm of concrete, which lets more heat through than the old figure said. They are now the largest loss through the walls in every home.

The heat each home loses on the coldest night, at −10 °C outside, per square metre of floor:

  • the grandparents' home: 51 → 42 W/m²;
  • the big family's home: 50 → 46 W/m²;
  • the caretaker's home: 38 → 37 W/m²;
  • the small duplex: 40 → 36 W/m².

The whole building needs about 18 kW instead of 20. These numbers now appear on the goals page, one for each home.

A question for the permit office

With the insulation outside, the street wall grows by about 0.1 m, out past the plot line. That is a question for the permit office, not a change to the design. If the office refuses, the street wall moves in by 0.1 m, and the street rooms lose 4.6 m² in all. No home gains or loses any floor today.
