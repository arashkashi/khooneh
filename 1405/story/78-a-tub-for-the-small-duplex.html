78. A tub for the small duplex

21 November 2026

On 17 November the owner noticed that the small duplex had no tub. The room optimiser looked, found no way to fit one without moving walls more than a little, and recommended keeping the shower room. On 21 November the owner settled it: "the small duplex does not have a bath tub, this is a hard rule, it should have one as well as a stand up shower please".

A rule for every home

The request became a hard rule, W11, for every home in the building. Each home must have at least one bath with a tub at least 1.6 m long and, in the same room, a separate stand-up shower at least 0.9 m square. The shower must be level where the home asks for one, and all four homes do. The owner can set the tub length anywhere from 1.5 to 1.7 m. The rule checks every home that has a bedroom, so a home with no bath at all would also fail. The grandparents', the caretaker's and the big family's homes already passed. The small duplex did not.

Searching for the floor

The duplex's shower room was 2.25 × 1.8 m. North of it is the core's concrete wall, and west of it the column at the landing. The washing machine had already moved out to the linen store. So the search moved the south wall, into the bedroom and the wardrobe alcove, from 0 to 0.8 m. It tried the door where it stands and 0.3 m further south, with the WC and without it, and every tub from 1.5 to 1.7 m. It found:

  • The alcove alone, even at 0.8 m, never holds a 1.6 m tub. At 0.6 m a 1.5 m tub fits, but only if the WC goes.
  • Moving 0.55 m into both still holds only a 1.5 m tub.
  • Moving 0.6 m into both is the least that holds a 1.6 m tub, and it keeps the WC. Leaving the WC out saves nothing.
  • Taking the hall strip south of the column gave 4.3 m², still no tub, and narrowed the way to the bedroom.

What was built

The bath is now 2.25 × 2.4 m: 5.4 m² instead of 4.05. The 1.6 m tub lies along the new south wall. The level shower and the WC stay where they were, and a full-size 0.6 m basin stands beside the tub. A 1.7 m tub would also fit, but then only a 0.5 m basin would.

The floor came from inside the duplex, so the home is no smaller:

  • The bedroom gave 0.72 m² and is now 11.6 m². It keeps its double bed with a way to both sides. Its corner south of the bath is part of the same room, so the room is now L-shaped. The plan learned to draw a room as more than one rectangle.
  • The wardrobe alcove gave 0.63 m². It is 2.2 m deep now and keeps its 2 m rail.

The slab's recess for the wet floor follows the larger bath. Its towel radiator is now the larger one the other main baths have. One check now flags the bedroom: at 11.2 m² of open floor it is just under the 11.5 m² that couple rooms aim for. That is a cost of the tub, and the owner can see it on the card.
