The story

90. Rain down the risers

22 November 2026

The roof garden had five drains, but no riser counted their downpipes, so nobody had checked that the pipes fit. R3 on −1 and −2 also left out the rainwater pipe that comes down to the rain tank. Two gaps found on 22 November, closed by the architect.

Where a downpipe can go

Each of the five drains was measured against every way down. The side walls are party walls, so no pipe can run outside on them. The street and yard faces have windows on every floor, and the yard face has balconies too. A drain away from a riser would need a pipe across home B's ceiling under the roof. Only two of the five stood within reach of a riser. So the roof now has three drains, one on the top of each riser. Each downpipe drops inside its riser, and the roof's falls run to them. The farthest roof point is now 9.5 m from its drain along the falls; with five drains it was 7.6 m. That means about 30 mm more screed at the far corner, at 1.5 %. The structural engineer checks the load. The stair-house roof spills into R1's drain through a rainwater head.

Sized from the roof

Each drain's share of the roof is measured along the falls, round the roof lights: 36 m² (R1, with the stair-house), 26 m² (R2) and 73 m² (R3), 136 m² in all. With a design storm of 100 mm an hour (a value for the services engineer to confirm from the national rainfall table), the largest flow is 2.0 l/s. A 75 mm pipe carries 4.0 l/s a third full (EN 12056-3), so every downpipe is 75 mm, lagged like the soil stacks. R1's and R2's pipes turn at the risers' feet on −1 and run under the shared ceilings to R3's foot. There they join the 100 mm pipe down to the −2 tank, which carries 3.8 l/s of its 10.7.

What it cost

R1 and R3 hold their pipe with room to spare. R3 had 46 % spare and is now no longer flagged as too large. R2 fell to 4.5 % spare on its top three floors, under the 5 % the owner asked for. The planner moved its north face out by its least step, 0.05 m, which gives 12 % spare; one step less fails. That takes about 0.04 m² on each level (floor audit): A −0.04 m², B −0.08 m² (the nooks on F3 and F4), C −0.04 m², D 0, and the shared floor −0.06 m² (−1's open middle and the ground-floor planter strip). The goals stay at 71.5.

A new rule

Hard rule RS-rain: every roof drain has a downpipe of a standard size that carries its roof's rain, standing on its riser (or on an outside face that is not a party wall). The riser stands on every floor down to its foot, and its foot leads to the rain tank (or to a sewer transfer the services declare). The riser holds the pipe on every floor with the 5 % reserve. The rainfall intensity, runoff, filling, how far a drain may stand off its riser, and the longest fall run can all be tuned.
