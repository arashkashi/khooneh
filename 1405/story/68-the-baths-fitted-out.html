The story

68. The baths fitted out

21 November 2026

The owner looked inside the baths and found them empty: "please include the shelving for the interiors of the showers and toilets, the mirror the cabinet etc. also I need a rule and config the washer in the small bathrooms should be as big as the master bathrooms. should have a mirror, and also cabinet and place for garbage bin, storage of the washing tools and liquids, mops etc. maybe some of them could go into the general storage in the unit." The architect read "the washer" as the wash basin: the mirror and the cabinet are in the same sentence, and the washing machines are one and the same machine everywhere.

Everything a bath holds

Until now a bath had its five big fixtures, a towel rail, a tiled niche and a mirror cabinet that existed only as a note. Now every one of the ten wet rooms is fitted out by the planner and measured. Each shower has a steel shelf of two tiers in a corner of its back wall, at 1.2 and 1.5 m, wide enough for the bottles of the people who wash there: eight bottles for most showers, twelve for the big family's children's bath. Each tub has a shelf over its rim. Over every basin hangs a mirror cabinet as wide as the basin, and under it stands the vanity cupboard; together they hold 45 to 120 litres. Beside every WC a roll holder is on a wall within half a metre of the seat, the spare rolls are in the vanity, and the brush stands at the foot of the wall behind the pan. Every person who bathes in a room has a towel place within a step of the shower or tub, a rail and a row of hooks; a WC has a hand towel by its basin. And every room has a place for a pedal bin, on free floor within a metre of the basin or the WC, never in the space in front of a fixture, beside a WC's seat or in a door's way. In the small changing room on -1 the only free floor is under its wall-hung basin, so the bin goes there.

Basins as big as the main bath's

The new hard rule BA-basin compares every basin with the main bath's in the same home, and sets a least size for all. Two WCs took the 0.6 x 0.5 m basin of the main baths at once: the big family's on its top floor, and the grandparents' shower room by their entrance, whose level shower gave up 10 cm of its length for it. The small duplex's guest WC seemed not to: the furniture search keeps 0.7 m clear inside every door, and a 0.5 m deep basin ran 5 cm into that. The search offered a semi-recessed basin 5 cm shallower instead. But the rules themselves ask for 0.3 m in front of a door, and the owner asked for "as big as", so the architect placed the full basin on the west wall by hand: every rule passes. The changing room on -1 keeps its 0.5 m basin: in a room the owner wanted as small as possible, a wider one would face into the shower, stand in front of the WC or in the door's way.

The surprise was the grandparents' own bath. Its basin was the smallest in the building, 45 cm, squeezed between the door and the tub the day before by the rule that keeps doorways clear. Moving the basin alone found no place. With every fixture free, the search found one arrangement that keeps all five and passes every rule: the tub along the north wall, the level shower in the corner, the basin in the alcove, the WC facing the room, and the 1.5 m turning circle still there. The room optimiser scores it higher than the old layout. The door then slid a little along its wall, and the hall's laundry basket and an extra cupboard of the day before were no longer needed where they stood.

Mops, liquids and rolls

The household's list of things gained the baths' cleaners, one set for each bath and WC, kept in its own vanity or in the home's utility cupboard within six metres on the same floor; a stock of toilet rolls kept by the baths; and bin bags. The mop and bucket, the broom, the vacuum and the cleaning liquids already had their places in the utility cupboards and stores, and keep them.

On the plans the high shelves and hooks are drawn dashed above the cut, the bins and brushes as small symbols, and every piece names itself under the pointer. No room grew or shrank; the bins and brushes take between 0.01 and 0.23 m² of each home's bath floor, and the grandparents' hall gets 0.36 m² back.
