The story

44. The round of 17 November

17 November 2026

17 November was a long day. Several pieces of work ran side by side and were joined into one design in the evening: furniture that belongs together, every stair measured, the pipe shafts, the appliances, A's bathroom, the concrete frame, the sky at the windows, the goals index and the question queue. At the end you answered two questions. This chapter is the round as a whole: what changed, how the scores moved, and what is still yours to decide.

Your answer on small rooms

On 9 November you wrote that a room with a bed should be at least 10 m², or not have a bed. A space under 10 m² should have no wall or door of its own, but should still be seen as a space. On 17 November you chose option B: both parts, and the small bedrooms grow.

  • B's two children's rooms grew 0.9 m into the play hall: 7.7 to 10.0 m² and 7.9 to 10.3 m². The children will be teenagers in this home, and a teenager needs a door that closes.
  • A's and C's second rooms took 0.48 m from the couple room: 8.6 to 10.1 m². The couple rooms went from 13.5 to 12.0 m², still above the 11.5 m² they need. They now keep one 2.1 m wardrobe and no separate bedding cupboard; the bedding goes on the wardrobe's top shelves. With that, the bed has room on both sides, and the wardrobe no longer stands in front of the window.
  • The window of the couple room moved 0.2 m west, so it clears the new wall. The same window moved on every floor, so the street face keeps its straight columns of windows.
  • A's store has a curtain instead of a door. Every small open space now has a mark of its own: a raised window seat in the reading corner, a stone floor to where shoes come off in each entry, a tiled floor in each kitchen, a curtain over the tea corner's shelves.

The play hall paid for this. It is now 11.0 m², with its corner by the reading corner, against 15.9 m² before. It is still the biggest of B's rooms on that floor, but D's bedroom on the same floor is bigger (11.8 m²). At 1.8 m deep it no longer holds a sofa or a table for four; floor cushions and the play rug stay. Four doors can no longer rest flat against a wall when open: the two second rooms' doors, and the two bathroom doors beside the moved couple-room doors.

Your answer on the kitchens needed no change to the plan. The rooms, wardrobes and bathrooms stay sized for the people who sleep there, and every everyday table and kitchen is sized for at least four people at the table.

How the scores moved

The goals index gives the whole design a score out of 100. At the start of the round it was 71.8; now it is 72.0. "Comfortable homes" went from 81.5 to 82.9: A from 89.0 to 93.3, B from 86.7 to 90.9, C from 89.3 to 93.6, because the couple and children's rooms lost their dead corners and their blocked ways. A's light went down a little, 65.0 to 64.7, with the moved window. The checks the design must pass are where they were: four fail, 38 ask for a fix. But they now point at 184 places instead of 211.

The design page's first section, "The design as it stands", now shows this scorecard in English and Persian. It compares the start of the round with the design as it is built, so it stays true after every change.

Progress you can see

You asked for the pipeline to show its progress. The steps already had a bar and the time left. Now the tests have one too: "tests 12 of 86, 1 minute 20 seconds so far, about 2 minutes to go", worked out from how long each test took last time.

What waits for you

55 questions are open. The question queue asks them one at a time, most important first. The first five are where the pipe shafts go, D's upper floor, B's bathroom on the terrace, the widths of the doors, and the concrete walls across the plot. Two older questions, on the couple rooms' wardrobes and on B's living room, still describe the rooms as they were before this round; their drawings show that earlier plan.
