The story

31. Numbers before pictures

10 November 2026

On 10 November, while the round-5 pictures were still being made, you looked at the renders and saw that some of what they show is decided only in the 3D model. The lamps, the things on shelves, the plants, the neighbours' blocks: nothing in the plan data places or checks them, and no drawing shows them. You asked for the order to be turned the right way round: first the numbers and a 2D drawing, then a 3D model that only follows them.

What you asked for

  • Furniture drawn in detail, from its own parts. Not boxes: pillows, cushions, chairs, the sink bowl, the hob, the WC pan, the tub's inner edge, wardrobe doors. The same parts the 3D model builds from, so the drawing and the model can't drift apart. The decision pages too.
  • A 2D drawing of every floor, from −2 to the roof, at a printable scale, with walls, doors and their swings, windows, furniture, room names and areas, dimensions and north.
  • Furniture that knows its neighbours. How far each piece is from the next, which way it faces, whether its door opens: a fridge door, an oven door, a wardrobe, a drawer. A fridge in front of a large window makes no sense. These become numbers and rules, and the furniture search plans around them, not only checks afterwards.
  • Rooms that may grow, for a good reason. When a room can't take what it needs, the search may propose moving a light wall into a neighbour with room to spare. The proposal comes to you as a question, with the numbers behind it.
  • The services, from the layout. Drains, vents, water, gas, cooling and the risers they go through, worked out from where the baths and kitchens are, with their own drawings.
  • The work in layers. Each layer rests on the one before: the site, the building's mass, the structure and everything that runs through every floor, rooms and walls, openings, fittings and services, furniture, details, and only then the pictures.

What we answered

The layers are right, with three corrections. A foundation is built first but sized last, because it carries everything above it; so the ground comes first, and the foundation's size at the end. The columns belong with everything else that crosses every floor: the stair and lift, the risers, which rooms are wet, the depth of the floor slab. And the layers are not a one-way street: when a lower layer fails, it sends a proposal up, never a silent change.

One fault showed straight away: the middle riser is 0.6 m wide on three floors and 0.3 m on the others. A shaft that narrows can't carry one pipe from top to bottom. Today it is a question for the engineer; in the new model it is a failed check.

Design first, and services that fit

You put the design slightly ahead of the services, and asked for some creativity so that both get a clean job. The ideas we will test in numbers:

  • A thick service wall that serves both its sides, with niches in the old way: pipes behind, shelves in front.
  • Risers against the columns, the stair and the lift, in corners no one would furnish.
  • Your raised platforms, and B's soffeh, carrying the fall a drain needs, so a bath can stand further from its riser.
  • Baths aired by a sun-warmed shaft or a windcatcher on the roof, with fewer fans.
  • Only dry services in the brick fins of the facade, where they also shade the south-west glass; water stays inside, away from frost.
  • Cooling by small pipes instead of large ducts, so the risers can be slimmer.
  • Pipes never run through the ceiling of another home.

The design's score stays the leading one. The services must work, and they add a small cost to the score; where there are good choices either way, you'll see them side by side.

Where it stands

The furniture drawings and the floor sheets are being built now, kept apart from the round-5 pictures until those are finished. Then come the layers, then the services and the furniture's neighbours side by side, and last, moving everything the 3D model now places on its own into the plan data, with a check that the 2D and the 3D agree.
