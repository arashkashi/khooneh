The story

75. The heat, room by room

21 November 2026

With the building's skin written down in one file (chapter 74), the next question was what keeps each room warm, and how big the boiler has to be. Until now neither had been sized against anything. The services named a boiler of about 60 kW. The corner review had placed floor heating and towel radiators, and judged every room against one flat figure, 60 W for each square metre of floor.

Every room's own number

The heat calculation now runs in the pipeline, as its own step, every time the plans change. For every heated room it measures what lies beyond each wall, under the floor and over the ceiling: the street, the yard, a balcony, the unheated stair, the car park, the roof. From that it works out how much heat the room loses on the coldest night, at −10 °C outside. The answers range from 14 W per square metre, in the inner rooms of the caretaker's home and the small duplex, to 107 in the big home's corners on the street and under the roof. A single figure for all of them had been too high for most rooms and too low for a few.

The heat each home needs on that night, and over a winter before the sun and the people add their own:

  • the grandparents' home: 4.8 kW, about 8,400 kWh a year;
  • the big family's home: 7.8 kW, about 13,700 kWh;
  • the caretaker's home: 2.9 kW, about 5,200 kWh;
  • the small duplex: 2.4 kW, about 4,200 kWh.

Four rooms that needed a little more

Of the 44 heated rooms, 40 are warm enough with what was already placed. Four, all in the big family's home, were not:

  • the homework corner at the street window;
  • the play hall's small corner against the stair;
  • the coat bay by the front door, whose shelves cover much of its floor;
  • the couple's bathroom under the roof, which has no floor heating and lost more than its towel radiator gives.

The architect, deciding for the owner, gave the first three a small panel radiator, 0.4 m long, on a wall that was already free. The bathroom gets a second towel radiator beside the shower. Nothing else moves and no home loses floor. The big home's kitchen is warm enough with the heater already in its cupboard plinth, so the second plinth heater the gaps study suggested is not added.

A smaller boiler

The four homes need 17.9 kW together. Reheating a 300 litre hot-water store in an hour, after the evening's showers, needs another 17.5 kW, so 35.4 kW in all. A new rule checks that the boiler gives between 1 and 1.5 times that. The old 60 kW would be 1.7 times, the size an uninsulated building wants. A boiler that big would switch on and off all winter and waste gas. The design now has a 40 kW boiler.

These are an architect's estimates, made so the design can be compared and checked. The services engineer sizes the real system, and both decisions are marked for the engineer to confirm.
