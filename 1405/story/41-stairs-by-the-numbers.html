The story

41. Stairs by the numbers

17 November 2026

You asked on 16 November: "please review the stairs, make sure the number of stairs and elevation change and resting space is standard, make it a numerical thing." You then added that the rules must reach every stair and step the building has or will have, so that none can escape the check.

One list of every stair

The project now keeps one register of stairs, built from the plan data. It finds each stair, step and raised floor on every level. A separate check looks for every change of height in the plan: floors that people use, the floors of each two-storey home, the court against the ground, raised built-ins, and rooms drawn as stairs or steps. That check does not use the register, and a test fails if it finds a change of height the register has missed. The register holds eleven entries for 1405:

  • the shared stair, seven storeys from −2 to the roof;
  • D's spiral, from the second floor to the third;
  • B's own stair, from the third floor to the fourth;
  • the court stair, from the court floor up to the bridge;
  • B's soffeh, a raised floor 0.40 m high.

1405 draws no threshold step and no ramp. For 1399 the register also lists the jacuzzi steps and the sunken pit, both left open because the data does not give their heights.

Fourteen rules

Each stair is worked out from its numbers:

  • how many equal risers divide the floor height;
  • a going taken from the middle of the step rule (two risers and a going add up to 630–640 mm);
  • whatever length the box leaves over, which becomes landings.

Fourteen rules then test every stair of the kinds they apply to: riser, going, step rule, risers per flight, landings, headroom, width, guards, handrails, even risers, spiral, door swing, open risers and raised floors. Each figure is a setting you can change, with its source. Iran's figures come from summaries of Parts 3 and 4 of the National Building Regulations, because the official text could not be found online, so they are labelled as secondary sources. DIN 18065 and the UK's Approved Document K sit beside them for comparison.

What the numbers say

  • The shared stair passes on every count but one. Its risers are 173 to 180 mm (17 to 20 a storey, in flights of 8 to 10), its goings 280 to 288 mm, and its half landings 1.10 m deep. Floor landings are 1.33 to 1.66 m, and headroom is at least 2.23 m, the lowest point being under the stair house on the roof. The exception: one source quotes a 2.40 m stair shaft, and the box is 2.30 m. Card wip-st-core-box recommends keeping 2.30 m until the engineer reads the printed clause.
  • B's stair passes. It has 18 risers of 178 mm, 280 mm goings and two flights of 9 risers, 0.90 m wide as you asked. Its half landing is 0.90 m deep, and it has at least 3.8 m clear above it, under the roof.
  • D's spiral fails on its treads. On the walking line each tread is 128 mm deep, where 200 mm is asked. A bigger box would not help; only turning further does. Card wip-st-spiral recommends 16 risers of 200 mm turning about 460° in the same box.
  • The court stair now passes. A 288 mm going replaces 280 mm, and the half landing is still 1.24 m.
  • The soffeh is a seat, not a step: at 0.40 m it falls within the seat band.

What was fixed, and what was not

Three things were matters of calculation and are now fixed in the numbers:

  • the way each floor height is split into risers;
  • goings that had been stretched to fill each box, which made the shared stair's goings 0.39–0.43 m long;
  • the end of the core stair that meets its landing.

Two plan matters became cards instead of silent edits: the spiral's treads and the core box's width. Some differences remain for the 3D model to catch up on when it is next rebuilt:

  • it still stretches the goings;
  • it starts the core flights at the street end;
  • it puts a 1.00 m guard round openings where 1.10 m is asked;
  • it measures floors with a 0.30 m slab, while the page's clear heights assume a 0.48 m build-up.

Your open-riser stairs fit the rules. The two homes' own stairs keep open risers, with a 40 mm bar under each tread so that a 100 mm ball cannot pass. The shared escape stair keeps closed risers.
