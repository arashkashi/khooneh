The story

79. The tub under the window

21 November 2026

The couple's bath on the big family's top floor is 8.6 m², on what used to be the street terrace. As drawn, it held everything along its edges: a basin under the window, a second basin on the west wall, the shower in one corner, the tub along the south wall and the WC beside it. The middle of the room was empty and nothing in it looked at the window. The owner looked at it and wrote: "the arrangement of this items in the bathroom could be much different please. please be creative and improve it. I mean the bathtub could be next to the window maybe? just as idea."

Nine layouts, each one built

The architect studied three directions. The first put the tub at the window. The second gathered the water into one wet zone. The third let the bath planner search freely. Each layout was built in its own copy of the whole design, and every rule was run on it: the clear space in front of each fitting, the sweep of the window's sashes, the neighbour's view, the drains' fall to the riser inside the wet floor, the way through the room. Three layouts failed. One moved the door and made a bedroom window climbable. One lost the separate shower. One was never measured. The others passed, but some left dead pockets of floor, and one met the owner's idea only with the tub's head.

What was built

A 1.6 m tub stands centred under the street window, its back to the north wall, both casements over the water. The level 0.9 × 0.9 m shower is in the south-east corner, behind glass. The hung WC is on the west wall just past the door, on the door's own wall, so it is out of sight as you come in. Two basins with mirror cabinets stand side by side on the south wall and face the light. From the door you see the tub under the window and the glass shower beyond it. There is a towel rail at the tub's head and hooks by the shower, and a shelf under the sill.

The room keeps its walls and its door, so no home gains or loses floor. The free floor grows from 5.67 to 5.75 m², and the largest free circle from 1.43 to 1.52 m. The tub's waste runs 6.35 m to the riser and needs 0.187 m of the 0.22 m the wet floor allows. Two drains that ran over 6 m before are now shorter, and one pipe-length flag remains, the tub's.

Two small things the layout taught

The planner hung the towel rail where it fitted best, and with the tub under the window that would have been on the glass. It now has a rule of its own: a rail never goes on a window's glass, nor within a hand's width of its frame.

The window's note had always said frosted to 1.8 m, but the window type it used had no frosting. It now has its own type: satin laminated casements from the sill to 1.8 m, so the bath is not seen from across the street, with a clear hopper above for the sky and the air. And because the tub's rim stands only 0.35 m below an opening casement thirteen metres up, in a home with a two-year-old, the casements get a child-safety restrictor. They open 100 mm, and an adult releases them for cleaning. The hopper does the airing. That is the architect's decision, left open for the engineer and the owner.

What it costs

The tub has no end wall, so its taps stand on its deck. A short tiled ledge at its west end would carry wall taps, but it takes floor by the door, so it waits as a variant to be measured. The casement handle is a stretch over the tub. Lying in the water you see sky through the clear band at the top, not the street.
