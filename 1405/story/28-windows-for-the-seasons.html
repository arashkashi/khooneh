Chapter 28 · 9 November 2026 · done, awaiting the owner

Windows for the seasons

The owner asked for the windows to be adjusted with care, for rules on their heights, and for a building of the same size to be considered on the south side. Their thought was that if the building behind is tall, bigger windows and balconies on the lower floors might not change the light at all.

The AI first fixed the directions. The plot is turned 37°, so the street faces north-east and the yard south-west. The two long sides are party walls. Under the city's depth rule, a building our size on the plot behind stands on our rear boundary, 6.1 m from our yard face. It is 16 m high, with a stair house above.

The owner's guess held for sun, and not for light. On a clear 21 December only the fourth floor's yard glass gets real sun, about six hours. On the floors below, the building behind takes almost all of it, and each square metre of glass loses more heat over a winter than it gains. But those lower rooms still need the sky light. The surprise was the balconies: a 1.8 m balcony over a window took nearly all the sky the building behind leaves. A's kitchen saw 0.8% of the sky, and D's bedroom 1.2%. A shallower balcony did not help; only moving it off the window did.

Done (9 November), for the owner to keep or undo.
  • The first- and second-floor living rooms on the yard have their sills raised from 0.45 to 0.9 m. The band below was satin glass, below the working plane, and only lost heat. The clear view now starts at 0.9 m instead of 1.0 m.
  • D's balcony stops short of A's kitchen window below. A's kitchen goes from 0.8% to 5.5% of the sky, its daylight factor from 0.28% to 0.75%, and it gets two hours of sun on 21 March.
  • B's fourth-floor balcony starts at the column, clear of D's bedroom window below. D's bedroom goes from 1.2% to 13.1% of the sky, and its daylight factor from 0.38% to 1.06%. Both balconies keep their 1.8 m depth and their doors. Each is now about 5 m² instead of 9.9 m².
  • The fourth floor keeps its tall glass, because there the sun pays. The louvred shutters stay on every yard window. The summer heat arrives after 13:00, low from the west, under any horizontal overhang.
  • Nine window rules now check every build: sills by room, heads, glass against floor, room depth, sky, winter sun, summer shade, street privacy, and a hard rule for the guard at a low sill. The sky and sun rules flag only what our own building takes, not what the neighbour takes.
What it asks of the owner.
  • Keep the shorter balconies, or the full width with A's kitchen and D's bedroom nearly without sky?
  • Find out how far the real building behind stands, how tall it is, and which side its stair house is on. At 12 m the lower floors get one to four hours of winter sun, and their low glass would pay back a little.
