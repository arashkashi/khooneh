The story

50. The caretaker's kitchen

18 November 2026

You looked at the caretaker's kitchen on the design page and saw three things wrong. There were two hobs. The fridge door opened into a cupboard. And behind the stove there was a strange empty space. You asked for each mistake to be found in the numbers, turned into a rule, and never repeated in any home.

Two hobs: one in the data, two in the drawing

The kitchen held one hob. The page drew a second one because it looked each drawing up by the piece's place alone. One of the search's other layouts had a counter in exactly the same place, carrying a sink and a hob, and the page drew that counter instead of the chosen one. Ten pieces in the building shared a place this way. Now each drawing is looked up by everything it shows. A test checks that every piece on the page is drawn as itself, and that a kitchen shows a hob only where its data has one. A new rule counts the cooktops: one per kitchen.

The fridge door

The fridge stood in the small alcove beside the riser. The worktop began 10 cm in front of it, so its door met the cupboard after opening 10 degrees. The kitchen plan had already seen this: the large fridge the family needs fitted nowhere, so it kept the old fridge as drawn, without checking its door. Now every appliance door is swung, step by step, from shut to 90 degrees open, against cupboards, walls and columns. A door that can't open fully is refused. The search also chooses which side the hinge goes on, so the handle is beside a worktop. D's fridge now opens toward its counter. When the family's size of fridge fits nowhere, the search tries the next size down instead of keeping a fridge that doesn't work.

The space behind the stove

The side worktop stood against the riser, but its last 55 cm ran past the riser's corner, 30 cm in front of the column. That left a slot behind it that nobody could reach or clean. A new rule allows at most 5 cm between a worktop's back and its wall, and no shut pocket beside the kitchen's units. The search can now cut a worktop back to its wall, carry it on along its wall to the next wall, or fill a narrow gap behind its end with a cupboard. Here a 30 cm cupboard goes under the same worktop, opening to the living room.

What the caretaker's kitchen is now

The worktop on the bath wall now runs into the alcove up to the wall. A 70 cm fridge-freezer (330 + 108 litres) stands at its end beside the hall, with its door hinged on the hall side. The sink, the drainer and a 45 cm dishwasher are on that wall. The hob is on the riser side, so its hood duct goes straight into the riser. It has 80 cm of worktop on one side and 40 cm on the other. The week's walking score went from 4454 to 3658. Some soft faults remain: the kitchen is small for a family of three, the prep worktop is 80 cm where 120 is wanted, the tea station has no room, and the dishwasher is the slim size.

Earlier today the architect chose two tall columns for this family's fridge and freezer, standing in the hall. The new rules showed that the pull-out larder meant for the alcove in that choice could not open either. So the kitchen is built with the 70 cm fridge-freezer, and the choice is open for you to review: 588 litres in the hall, 3.2 m from the sink, or 438 litres in the kitchen.

The other kitchens

In A, the dishwasher sat in the corner, and its door came down onto the next worktop. It now sits between the hob and the sink, with its door opening into free floor. In D, the run now reaches the corner and the fridge's handle faces the worktop. B is unchanged. Every kitchen is checked by the same rules on every build. A test runs them over the real plan, so a repeat of any of these faults stops the pipeline.
