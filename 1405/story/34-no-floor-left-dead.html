34. No floor left dead

11 November 2026

Looking at the caretaker's bathroom on the design page, you saw two gaps: "There is a dead space on the left side of the bath tub, there is dead space between the sink and the washing machine." And you asked for more than a fix: "make some numerical rules to better place items here, and the rules you write and test to be applied elsewhere ... so we do not make the same mistake elsewhere."

Why the gaps were there

A 0.5 m deep column stands in the bathroom's west wall. It leaves two pockets, each 0.50 × 0.85 m, one on either side of it. The layout search let a fixture stand with half its back on the column and half on nothing. So the tub went against the column with the pocket behind it, closed off on every side. The basin went on the column too, and the washing machine, 0.6 m wide in a 0.85 m pocket, left a 0.25 m notch between them. Nothing in the search measured the floor behind or beside a fixture, only in front of it.

Four new rules, for every room

  • No dead strip (DS-strip). No free strip narrower than 0.30 m and longer than 0.30 m, and no pocket cut off from the door, unless it is the floor in front of a fixture, a WC's elbow room, a door's swing or a gap left on purpose for a pipe.
  • Flush or roomy (DS-snap). A fixture stands flush against a wall, a column or its neighbour (0.02 m), or far enough away to clean (0.30 m) or, for furniture, to step past (0.45 m). Nothing in between.
  • Little dead floor (DS-share). At most 4 % of a bath or WC, 5 % of a kitchen and 8 % of other rooms.
  • Fill what can't be closed (DS-fill). Where a gap must stay, the catalogue suggests something that fits it, such as a towel rail or an open shelf. Nothing is invented.

Every number is yours to tune in the rules file, and each is tested at both ends of its range.

The search now uses them

The layout search now tries positions flush with every wall end and column face. It counts the dead floor of every arrangement it tries, and it never leaves out a fixture to save floor. In the caretaker's bath, the tub now stands in the middle of the column face, so both pockets open onto the room. The WC sits in the south-west pocket, closest to the riser, and the washing machine and basin are on the east wall. Dead floor there went from 0.55 m² to 0.03 m². The cost: the basin and washer now drain 4.0–4.6 m to the riser, more than the 3 m guide (a soft check, for the engineer).

Across the building, dead floor fell from 7.1 m² to 1.8 m². The rules also found something worse. In B's children's bath, the basin and shower could only be reached through a 0.2 m gap between the WC and the washing machine. That is fixed too.
