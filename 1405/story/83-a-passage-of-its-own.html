The story

83. A passage of its own

21 November 2026

The owner looked at the walk through the big home's living floor once more and was not pleased: "on the workflow still there is no clear passage between the living room and the bed rooms in the 4th floor. the storage is in the middle and does not even have a door to go through ... I think maybe the storage could go into this space ... and the toilet could go into this space ... I have asked multiple times and you still make this mistake ... there seem to be something you are missing since you are repeating this issue."

What was being missed

Each earlier answer had been checked against the rule written for it. The connection rule (20 November) asked that the front door reach every room along halls. The clear-way rule (that morning) asked that those ways be halls, not storage aisles. The layout built that morning passed both. But to pass them it had sent the family round: from the living room up the entry hall, past a riser's box, through the family door and into the family hall, behind a walk-in pantry that stood exactly where the natural passage would be, in the middle of the floor. From the kitchen to the couple's room was 13.2 m. Neither rule ever asked the question the owner kept asking: are the day rooms and the bedrooms joined by a passage of their own?

The question as a rule

A new hard rule, MV-direct: on every floor of every home, each kitchen and the main living room reach every bedroom there by the family's own way. That way never runs through the room the front door opens into on to another hall (a bedroom may open off the entry hall itself, as the second rooms of the one-level homes do), is at most 14 m long (tunable, 12 to 20), and is a clear way as the clear-way rule measures one. Run on the plan as it stood, it failed on exactly one floor, B's top floor, from the living room and from the kitchen, and passed every other home. That is the test of a rule like this: it has to fail where the owner sees the fault, and nowhere else.

The passage

The owner's pictures did not reach the AI, so the layout follows the earlier hint the owner gave on this floor (the old air shaft's strip, and moving the small WC):

  • The pantry's place becomes the passage, the whole 1.85 m wide, straight from the kitchen end of the living room to the family hall, with the hinged family door at its far end and its side open to the entry.
  • The walk-in pantry moves east of it, into the old hall and the WC's place: 1.85 x 3.9 m, shelves on both long walls, its hinged door off the passage. It holds more than before (3,745 L of kitchen storage on the floor, 2,300 L needed).
  • The guest WC moves into the old shaft's strip off the entry, beside riser R1, where the coat and shoe bay was; a 60 mm step in the slab under it lets its drain fall inside the floor, and a small towel radiator warms it. The coats hang over the entry's shoe cabinet.

From the kitchen to the couple's room is now 9.8 m, up the passage; from the front door 9.9 m (it was 6.7, through the old hall), so the guests' way and the family's are one passage now. Nothing stored lines either way. B's usable floor rises a little, 131.28 to 131.59 m², and no hard rule newly fails.
