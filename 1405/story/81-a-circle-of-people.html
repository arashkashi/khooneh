The story

81. A circle of people

21 November 2026

The owner looked at the big home's top-floor living room and saw what the furniture said: everyone faces the wall or the TV. “There is no circle of the people. I know in many families TV is important but there should be possiblity of people sitting in a circle,” they wrote, and asked for it as a number, drawn in the plans and later in the 3D model.

Why the old check let it pass

A check for a sitting group existed since 20 November, but it counted pieces of furniture round the sofa, and it took two seats at a corner for two people facing each other. So the grandparents' room passed with the sofa looking at the TV and the armchair looking at the window, and the big home passed with its sofa 0.75 m from the soffeh, close enough for knees to meet and with no room for a glass of tea. It also never looked at the rooms laid out with floor cushions.

The rule now counts people

A sofa seats its people, an armchair or a pouf one, a soffeh or a row of floor cushions one person every 0.6 m. Each home's main living room passes when, with beds or on the floor, it seats the household and one guest:

  • within a circle at most 3.0 m across, everyone facing its middle (within 45 degrees);
  • with at least two people truly facing each other, at least 1.0 m between the fronts of their seats;
  • with a low table, or free floor for a sofreh, in the middle.

The TV may stay. The rule is a hard one, and each number is in the rules file with its range, so the owner can tune it. The halls, the big home's reading corner and the shared rooms downstairs are not judged; the owner wants the shared floor kept open.

Four living rooms

Three designers measured each room against the real rules before anything was built. Before, all four rooms failed.

  • The big home (fourth floor). The sofa moves 1.05 m back and faces the soffeh across a tray table, with a pouf on the third side: seven people in a 2.9 m circle, eight on the floor with the sofreh between the cushions and the soffeh. The TV slides 0.475 m along the wall so both seats face it. The move also opened the way from the balcony door.
  • The grandparents' home (first floor). A two-seat sofa faces the TV; an armchair at each end faces the other across the low table: four in a 2.6 m circle, on chairs, as the step-free home needs.
  • The caretaker's home. A smaller TV cupboard is centred on the sofa, and two poufs face it across the low table: five in a 2.7 m circle. The TV is now straight in front of the sofa, which it was not before.
  • The small duplex. The owner's own layout stays exactly. Two poufs under the window face the sofa across the low table: four in a 2.2 m circle. The poufs are low, so the sofa still looks at the yard.

The planners choose these arrangements because the rule prices any layout without a circle. No wall moved, and no home gained or lost floor.

Seen on paper and in 3D

The plans and the design page draw each circle as a light dashed ring through the seats, a dot for each person, labelled with how many sit there and how wide it is. The 3D model gets the poufs and a round rug under each circle, so the circle shows up in the pictures too.
