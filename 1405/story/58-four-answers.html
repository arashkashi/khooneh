The story

58. Four answers, and a missing rule

19-20 November 2026

Late on 19 November you answered four questions about the big home. Each answer is now part of the design, and each one is measured.

A store under the stair

The floor audit had found floor under the big home's private stair, on the children's floor, that nothing used. The AI suggested leaving it open so the stair stays see-through. You chose the store instead. It sits under the returning flight and half the half landing: 3.1 m², 0.85 m deep, about 1.6 m high at the low end and 3 m at the top. A thin partition and a closed underside separate it from the stair. Its whole length opens to the passage behind a curtain, so everything in it can be reached from there, and the shelves step down as the stair comes down.

No floor was added and none was lost. The stair keeps its whole box and is drawn exactly as before. On this floor the stair's room is now only the first flight and the gap between the flights, so no square metre is counted twice. The big home stays at 171.7 m², and 3.1 m² of it is now storage. The new partition and the short wall to the play hall take 0.44 m² as wall.

Your own rules shaped the store. A small store gets a curtain, not a door (your 7 November wish, rules S6 and S-open-small). The rule allowing one walk-in store per home (S5) was the harder one, because the big home already has its pantry. That rule exists to keep open floor that people can shape. Floor under a flight, too low to stand in at one end, could never be that kind of floor. So the rules now treat a store under a stair as a cupboard, and the rule still holds for every other store.

A top floor in two halves: an error, and a new rule

On 19 November the AI moved the big home's top-floor front door so it opens onto the living room. In doing so it accepted that the couple's side of that floor (the family hall, the master bedroom and its bath) could be reached from the front door only through the walk-through pantry: about 12 m through the living room and between shelves. The only other way in was the private stair from the floor below. A soft check flagged the route, but nothing stopped it. That was the AI's error. You then chose a hinged door at the pantry's living-room end, so the family side could be closed off from guests.

On 20 November you saw the problem: "how someone would enter the unit and goes into the kitchen and then is able to walk to the master bed room on the 4th floor. the unit on the 4th floor seems to be divided into two separate part ... it seems some hard necessary movement and connection rule/config is missing." You were right. There is now a hard rule, MV-connect. On every floor of every home, every bedroom, living room, kitchen, study and bath must be reached from that floor's own front door. The way may run through halls, landings, the home's own stair and, by an allowance you can switch off, the living or dining space. It may never run through a store, a pantry, a bath or another bedroom. Halls must be at least 0.9 m wide, doors and openings at least 0.7 m, and no way may be longer than 20 m. You can tune each of these numbers. The rule is tested with planted faults at both ends of each range, and it counts in the goals. Run on every home, only the big home's top floor failed. The other homes, the small duplex's two floors and the children's floor all pass.

The fix goes through the plan, not by hand. The walk-through pantry becomes a hall, 1.0 m wide. It opens to the entry and to the living room, and the pantry runs along it as a curtained alcove beside the stair. The couple's bedroom is now about 9 m from the front door, and the way does not cross the living room. Your door now stands at the hall's far end, where it opens into the family hall: one hinged door still closes the family side off from guests. Its new place replaces the place you chose, because the pantry is no longer the way. The pantry shrinks from 6.4 m² to a 2.9 m² alcove with 3.2 m of shelves. No floor is lost: the walls that go give back 0.3 m².

Clothes in the play hall

The homework corner can later become the youngest child's room. It has space for a bed and the desk, but not for a wardrobe as well. You said the clothes can go in a wardrobe in the children's play hall. That is now part of the check. A 0.9 m hinged wardrobe fits against the bedrooms' wall, between the two bedroom doors, with room to open it and with every path through the hall kept clear. It is not built now; the place is kept for when it is needed.

Summer shade, drawn

You confirmed the shading set. Hinged louvred shutters, like the ones on the yard side, go on the street windows of the reading corner and the top-floor street bedroom. A 0.6 m shelf-like sunshade goes over the top floor's yard glass and its door. The windows data, the plans and the sheets now show them, and the summer-shade study measures the building with them in place. On a clear 15 July, the reading corner drops from 337 to 168 Wh of sun per m² of floor, the street bedroom from 296 to 144, and the living room from 253 to 190. Every room is now under the 200 limit, and 94% of the winter sun still comes in. That limit is now a rule (SH-summer-gain) that you can tune between 150 and 300. It is a soft rule, because it assumes someone closes the shutters.
