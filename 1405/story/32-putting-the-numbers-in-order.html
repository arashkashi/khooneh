The story

32. Putting the numbers in order

10 November 2026

Before the work in layers could start, the code that turns the plan into numbers needed tidying. You asked for it on 10 November: "put things in better order and factor out items into data structure", with one condition first: "we need unit tests before to make sure we do not break any past functionality". The AI first wrote a review of the whole numerical pipeline, had it graded independently (92 of 100), and then carried it out in small steps.

The safety net first

  • A test that builds the design twice. For every step, the design is built from scratch twice, once from the step and once from the step before, and every one of about a hundred output files is compared. A step that changes anything is undone.
  • Tests for the parts that had none. The plan author, the window study, the door and furniture search, the walls and furniture, the page, the roof garden and the iteration record are now each tested on their own, with faults planted at the edges. The two largest parts went from under half of their lines tested to over 90 %.

What changed, with nothing in the design changing

  • Render code no longer marks the numbers stale. A fix to the walks used to make seven numerical stages look out of date. That link is cut.
  • One place for each thing. One way to read and write the data files; one set of plan geometry, where before "overlap" meant an area in some files and a yes-or-no in others; one list each of which rooms count as indoors.
  • The programme as data. The levels, their clear heights, the homes, who sleeps where and the private stairs now live in one data file (programme-1405.json) instead of in five program files. "B for four or five people" is now a one-number edit there.
  • The old plan author is archived, so it can no longer overwrite the current design by accident.

Numbers that disagree: yours to decide

Some numbers were copied, and the copies no longer agree. The AI did not pick one: each is listed on the decision page wip-refactor-constants, with what changes if they are made one.

Where it stands

Next come the layers themselves: the structure and everything that runs through every floor as its own data, then the folders by layer, then the large parts split by layer. The plan is in design/research/pipeline-review.md, section 7.
