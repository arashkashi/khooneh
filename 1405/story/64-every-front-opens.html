64. Every front opens

21 November 2026

The owner looked at the caretaker's kitchen and asked a plain question: "here in house keeper unit does the kitchen cabinet doors open and close properly, we should check for all kitchen cabinets". Until now only the appliances' doors were measured: the fridge and the freezer swinging to ninety degrees, and the floor before the dishwasher and the oven. The cupboards themselves, the drawers and the wall units were drawn as boxes along the runs and never opened.

A rule that opens every front

The new hard rule, KZ-fronts-open, opens every front of every kitchen in the plan, at its own height. A cupboard door swings to 90 degrees, base or wall. A drawer comes out 0.55 m, a full runner and its handle, and someone needs 0.40 m of floor in front of it, 0.45 m wide, to stand and use it. The pull-out larder comes out its depth and its handle. The dishwasher's and the oven's doors drop flat, with 0.40 m to stand beside them or in front. A wall cupboard stays under the ceiling and the beams and out of a window's sash. At an inner corner a front keeps 0.05 m from the other run's fronts, for the handles and the knuckles. Fronts that are used together open together: the dishwasher with the sink base, the oven with the drawers beside it. Nothing may meet a wall, a column, a riser box, another run, a tall unit, the hood, a room door's swing, the table or its chairs. All six numbers are in the owner's rules file, each tested at both ends of its range.

What it found

Twenty-eight clashes, in four of the five kitchens. In the caretaker's kitchen the drawers at the west end of the sink run faced a nook only half a metre deep before riser R2: they could not come out, and nobody could stand there. The sink base's 0.6 m door swept over the end of the hob run whichever way it was hinged. The grandparents' kitchen had two blind corners whose doors opened into the next run, a drawer stack that met the side of the twin fridge, drawers sliding past the next run's handles, and wall cupboards drawn in front of the yard window, where the 3D model never built them and the casements, opening in, would hit them. The small duplex had a drawer by the fridge with nowhere to stand, a blind corner and wall cupboards before the window. In the big home the tea counter's drawers met the end of the hob run, the island's back cupboards opened onto the dining table 0.2 m away, and the tea counter's cupboards and shelves stood before the window. W's tea corner was clean.

How the planner answers

None of this was fixed by hand. The kitchen planner now opens each front it makes and, where one fails, tries in turn the other hinge side or a pair of leaves, a door for a drawer or a drawer for a door, a filler at an end, and last a blind corner with no front of its own. Wall units no longer stand before a window: the 3D model had always left them out there, so the storage counted is now the storage built. The everyday table keeps the floor behind an island's back cupboards free. The caretaker's nook got two narrow doors and the sink base two leaves. The grandparents' and the small duplex's corners became blind corners; the small duplex's is a blind-corner cupboard reached through the next counter's door. The big home's table moved 0.3 m east, which puts it 0.12 m beyond the four metres the serve rule allows.

What it cost, and the top row

Taking out the wall cupboards that stood before the windows, and the blind corners, left the small duplex 140 litres short of its household's bulk need on its own floor, which the essentials rule holds hard. So the wall cupboards now rise to 2.60 m, just under the lowest beam, with a top row for rarely used things. Against what was counted before: the grandparents' kitchen holds 1511 litres instead of 1951, the big home 1299 instead of 1429, the caretaker's 1488 instead of 1288, the small duplex 1431 instead of 1577. No worktop was lost. The trades are honest ones: the caretaker's daily plates sit further from the drainer now that the nook has doors, a week's kitchen walking rose in three homes, and the goals moved from 76.5 to 76.1. Each kitchen's trade is on its own card, open for the owner's review. No floor changed in any home.
