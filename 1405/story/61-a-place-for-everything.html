The story

61. A place for everything

20 November 2026

It began with a question about the big duplex: "in the master bedroom bath room, is there a wash machine? should there be one, and also in its kitchen is there a dish washing machine? there seem some rules missing if there is none." Then the small duplex, and then the thing nobody had drawn: "there should be also a place for a standing line dry in the every unit. maybe this space could be in balconies, in summer and should be inside in winter ... and also the laundry basket each unit should have one or two different laundry basket places." And the wider ask: "search in this semantic domain and see what else essential items have missed ... these are essentials and should be hard rules and configs for their optimizations."

What was missing

Every home had its washing machine and its dishwasher, one each, sized to the household. Nothing else of the laundry was there: no basket anywhere, no place to dry indoors, no ironing board, nowhere for a folded rack. The small duplex's washer stood in its kitchen a floor below its only bedroom, so the basket went down a spiral stair. And past the laundry: no place for the vacuum or the mop, the step ladder, the suitcases, the other season's clothes, the guests' mattresses, the heater and the fan, the Nowruz things. The kitchens' own contents were already counted item by item; what the kitchen sends out (rice and oil in bulk, the guest dishes) had a store in some homes and not in others. The list, measured, is in research/household-essentials-1405.md.

The laundry, measured

A lidded basket now stands on every floor with a bedroom: one in each single-floor home, two in the big duplex, one by the small duplex's bed. The small duplex's washer moved up to its linen store, beside the riser, five metres from the basket, and nothing goes on the spiral stair any more. In winter the washing hangs on a ceiling airer, six bars on a pulley over the washer's own bath or the one beside it, where the fan takes the damp out: it takes no floor at all. In summer the big duplex dries on its covered loggia beside its washer; the other three homes go up by the lift to the roof's drying yards, because their balconies are in the guests' sight. Each home has a place to iron. You asked whether the big duplex needs two washers. Measured, a second one would save the parents' basket one flight a week, and cost 0.78 m² of floor, a drain twice as long as allowed, a second machine, and your own rule of one washer a home. It keeps one.

Everything else

Every need has litres, the kinds of place that may hold it and how far it may be from where it is used: clothes in the sleepers' own wardrobes, the vacuum and the mop within fifteen metres, the suitcases and the other season in the home's locked cage on the lowest level. Where a need found no place, the planner put a tall cupboard where it serves best, pricing every square metre: a utility cupboard in the grandparents' hall and in the big home's play hall, and in the caretaker's home a utility cupboard by the door and a pantry in the hall, so its weekly kitchen things no longer go to the basement. A coat rail hangs over every shoe cabinet. Floor taken for good: 0.52 m² in the grandparents' home, 0.68 in the big home, 0.76 in the caretaker's, 0.16 in the small duplex.

Two more rules from the plans

Looking at the small duplex's entry you wrote: "the place for the shoes is sticking out into the entrance gate. should be rule which would prevent it. a hard rule". It was: the cabinet's last quarter stood past the end of its wall, in the way to the living room. Now a piece set against a wall must have the wall behind it, and nothing may stand in a doorway or a passage. The rule found one more piece in the building, the grandparents' bath basin, six centimetres into its door; it is a little narrower now.

The big home's kitchen storage

Then, after the big home's top floor had gained its new hall so that it is one home again, you wrote: "we lost the storage on the top duplex." It had: the pantry beside the kitchen kept a one-deep shelf along the stair wall, about a third of what it held, and the rest of the home's storage was now a floor below. Nothing had noticed. Now a home's kitchen storage must stay on the kitchen's floor, near it, and hold at least the household's need; the big home keeps at least what its pantry held before. A deeper alcove was measured and refused, because the new hall would narrow below the width the connection rule asks. What came back instead is tall cupboards in the family hall, a few steps across the hall from the kitchen.

And the plants: they draw with leaves now, and each knows the light it needs. Every living room has one, the small duplex's hanging by the window because its floor is full, and every balcony and terrace has an olive tree. On the design page a new layer shows every place a plant could go, coloured by its light.
