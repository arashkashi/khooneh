The story

45. An architect looks at 1405

18 November 2026

You asked for an independent review: an architect in Qazvin who had never seen the drawings, looking at the whole house as if you had hired him. He was to change nothing you had decided, and to say plainly what is good, what is weak and what is missing. This chapter is what he found, in his words where they help.

What he thought of the house

"This is a Persian house, carried into today. It doesn't copy one. It turns a quiet face to the street and opens its living rooms to the yard and the court. The birch stands in the court on natural ground, with water in a stone basin beside it. There is a soffeh in the big family's living room. The roof is one the family works and sits on, as the old roofs of Qazvin were. Under it all is a plain concrete frame: two lines of columns, two party walls and one core. The pipes stack from the bottom to the top. I would build it."

His complaints were not about the rooms. They were about what keeps a building safe, warm and lawful, and about the drawings a builder and the permit office need.

What he found, most important first

  1. The only stair is open to the cars at ground level. In a car fire, smoke would rise past every front door. One fire door in an opening that already exists, closers on the four front doors, and a vent at the top of the stair make it safe. No wall moves.
  2. No sections and no elevations. The heights of the floors, the parapets and the court are on no sheet. These are the first drawings the permit office asks for.
  3. Nobody has drawn the gas, the meters or the heating. Every kitchen cooks on gas, but no pipe brings it, and there is no meter at the street and no chimney for a boiler. He proposes a meter cabinet at the street end of the planter strip, clear of the earthquake walls, the gas pipe on the yard face, and one boiler in the plant room with its chimney to the roof.
  4. Two terraces sit on top of rooms with no room for waterproofing. These are B's covered terrace over the caretaker's living room, and B's street terrace over the children's floor. He proposes setting their concrete down about 18 cm, so the door stays level and the water stays out.
  5. The earthquake walls, the gap to the neighbours, and the riser that cuts a beam. Your cards already ask about these. He gave his choice on each: thicker core walls with the stair's street wall, the party walls stepped in from the third floor, and the riser moved, not the column.
  6. Guests see the bedroom doors first. In A, C and B, the front door opens at the bedroom end of the home. "The old house had a hashti: you stepped in, turned, and walked a dalan to the talar, and the family's rooms were never the first thing you saw." He proposes a wooden lattice screen, 2.2 m high, between the entry and the couple room and bath. The guest room stays by the door, where it belongs. "It costs no wall, no door and no floor, and the hall still borrows light through it."
  7. A's home is step-free except at the roof. The roof door has a 15 cm step, though the lift goes up and A has a garden plot there. He proposes a level threshold with a drain. He also asks for a lift car long enough for a stretcher, if the lift maker can fit one in the same shaft.
  8. Footsteps over a neighbour's bed. D's bedroom is under B's dining table for eight. The caretaker's living room is under the children's play terrace. A floating floor on those two floors is what keeps neighbours friends.

What was fixed at once

  • In the 3D model, some pot plants stood inside columns, some balcony chairs inside the parapets, and B's rug ran under a column. The drawings marked 22 of them with red crosses. A new rule now keeps every such piece clear of the structure, and you can tune it. The crosses are gone.
  • On the caretaker's floor sheet, the kitchen's name ran under the pipe shaft's box. Names now step aside from a shaft or a column standing in the middle of a room.
  • One file was read by a path written by hand. It now goes through the project's single list of where files are.

What happens next

You asked for fewer questions, so the architect made the bigger choices himself. They are written as eight proposals at the top of his review (design/research/architect-review-1405.md), each with its numbers, ready to be built into the plan once the other work on the plan is joined. None of them changes the mix of homes, the shape of the building, the stair and lift, the court, or anything you decided.

The score did not move, and it shouldn't have: the fixes were to the 3D dressing and the drawings, which no goal measures. The proposals will move "safety" and "services", today the two lowest goals, once they are built and measured.
