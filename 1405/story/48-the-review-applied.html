The story

48. The review applied

18 November 2026

An independent architect looked at 1405 (chapter 45) and found a good, Persian house whose weak points were not in the rooms but in what keeps a building safe and running. You asked for the review to be applied to the design, and for the architect to decide on your behalf. This chapter is that round. Each decision is listed with the others taken for you, and any of them can be changed.

A stair you can escape by

The one shared stair serves eight levels, and at ground level it stood open to the walkway beside the four cars. A car fire is the likeliest fire in a building like this, and its smoke would have risen past every front door. Now a steel fire door hangs in that opening. In daily life a magnet holds it open, flat against the wall, so the walkway is as wide as before. On the fire alarm it shuts. The same kind of door closes the boiler room and the stores at the bottom of the stair. Every front door gets a closer and a fire rating, the lift's doors are fire-rated, and the top of the stair gets a smoke vent. Two new rules check this on every build: the stair is closed off from everything but the open air, and every door onto it shuts by itself.

Drawings a builder can read

The storey heights, the parapet, the court's depth and the facades were on no sheet. Now two sections, four elevations, a site sheet without any address and the door and window schedules are drawn from the same data as the plans. When a height changes, they redraw themselves.

Gas, meters and a boiler

Every kitchen had a gas hob, but nothing brought gas to it. Now one meter cabinet stands at the street end of the planter strip, clear of the concrete walls the frame needs. The gas pipe climbs the yard facade in the open air, where a leak can't collect, and goes into each kitchen. One condensing boiler in the plant room heats every home, each with its own heat meter. Its flue runs along the lowest ceilings into the supply riser and rises inside it to a metre above the stair-house roof. It never goes through the closed box beside the lift, which once was the night-cooling shaft (chapter 47). The supply riser still has room: 0.565 of its 0.6 m is used.

Terraces without a step

B's covered terrace, the children's playground, stands over the caretaker's living room. Its insulation and waterproofing need about 0.18 m, and the door had a 20 mm threshold. Now the slab under the terrace steps down 0.18 m. The children walk out with no step, and the caretaker's living room keeps 2.74 m of height under it. B's old street terrace is B's bath since the last round, so there the slab steps down 0.15 m as a wet floor.

A hashti at the door

In the caretaker's home and on B's top floor, the front door opened at the bedroom end. A guest at the door looked straight at the couple room's and the bath's doors. A walnut lattice, about half open, now stands on the entry's west line. It hides those two doors from the open front door, as the hashti of an old house turns the guest away from the inner rooms. The guest room stays by the door. The step-free home on the first floor has no screen. There it would have narrowed the wheelchair's way beside the bath door to 0.92 m, and made the night walk from the carer's room to a WC 8.4 m long. Its doors stay quiet and flush instead.

Level to the roof, and the lift

The roof door had a 0.15 m step, though the lift goes to the roof and A has a plot there. Now it is level. A covered channel drain outside takes the water, and the brick paving sits on adjustable pedestals. The review also asked for a lift car long enough for a stretcher. Measured, it does not fit. The shaft is 2.30 by 1.70 m between its concrete walls, and a 2.1 m car with its door and clearances needs about 2.45 m. The shaft could only grow by cutting a shear wall or taking the stair, so the car stays 1100 by 1400 mm. The lift maker is asked whether a 2.0 m car fits, and an evacuation chair is kept by the lift.

Quiet floors

A concrete slab alone lets footsteps through at about 72 decibels. The rule written for the review found more than the review named: the second floor's homes stand over A's rooms too. Now a floating screed on a resilient layer lies under every home's floor that is over another home, so footsteps reach the room below at about 50 decibels. The layer goes under the screed that was already allowed for, so no room loses height.

Changing any of it

Every decision is listed on the decisions page and on the design page, with what was chosen and why. To change one, paste the line shown there into the chat, for example "Decision wip-review-p5-hashti: option C".
