88. One way up

22 November 2026

Chapter 87 found that the plant room's way up was too small. The flue's own casing in the −1 foyer stood 0.10 m into the landing's door, leaving 0.62 m clear. R3's foot, the opening the pipes rise through, was 0.20 × 0.50 m clear and could not hold them. The owner had already handed such choices to the architect (21 November), so the architect chose option A of card wip-plant-way-up and built it.

One box for everything

The flue, the eight heating pipes (a flow and a return for each home) and the pumped cold main now rise together through R3's foot. Below the ground floor, R3 is one straight box on −2 and −1, 0.85 × 0.80 m (0.75 × 0.70 m clear). It stands west of the landing's wall and against −2's edge, where the retaining wall holds the slab. The casing in the foyer is gone, and the landing's door is back to 0.72 m clear. The flue keeps its own fire-rated wrap and its own fixings beside the pipes.

Sized by the packing, not by eye

The card guessed about 0.85 × 0.7 m. The packing model said no. That size holds the flue, the heating and the cold main with only 3% to spare, under the 5% the owner asked every riser to keep ("a very small amount"). It also forgot two pipes. The rain tank's rainwater pipe comes down R3 to −2, and its drip-water main goes back up R3 to the court and the roof beds. Both pass through the same slab opening.

The planner now counts those two pipes as well: any route that ends at a riser's foot goes on up through that riser's opening. Rule RS-casing now also asks a riser's slab opening for RS-snug's 5% reserve. With all twelve pipes counted, 0.75 × 0.70 m clear packs them with 7% to spare. That is snug, as the owner wanted, but not too small.

What it costs

No home loses floor. The floor audit measures the shared floor below ground at 0.68 m² less in use:

  • −2's stores lose 0.52 m².
  • −1's open middle loses 0.32 m².
  • The foyer and the landing gain 0.17 m²: the casing's 0.12 m², and R3's old tail on −1, which stood over earth and held nothing.

The structural engineer checks the slab opening. It sits at the edge of the −1 floor panel, on the retaining wall, with no column or beam in it.

The flue's length

Rising in R3 moves the flue's way up 0.65 m west, away from R1. The flue now runs 4.05 m on −2 and 3.60 m on −1: 7.65 m in all with six bends, where it was 7.55 m. That is 0.35 m under rule RS-gas-flue's 8 m. It is close to the limit, and boiler makers count each bend as extra length. The card therefore says plainly that the services engineer must check the boiler's equivalent flue length. If it is too long, card option C, the boiler on the stair-house roof, is still there.

The strip behind the lift

Card wip-fl-minus2-edge wants to run −2's landing over the 0.3 m strip behind the lift. The widened riser stands west of the landing's wall line, and the flue no longer rises through that strip. The two do not collide, and both cards now say so.
