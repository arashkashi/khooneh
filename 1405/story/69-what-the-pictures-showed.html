The story

69. What the pictures showed

21 November 2026

The owner looked through the first key pictures of the new design and wrote: "review the rendered pictures some of them are dark, and also find the clear issues in the pictures and fix the 2d and 3d models and maybe also add some rules configs to the model". All 164 pictures were looked at again, one by one, and measured.

Why some were dark

Every room had been given one lamp, an 18 W downlight or a 35 W ceiling disc, whatever its size, and a room with any window ran it at half power. A small nook got fifteen watts a square metre; the big home's living room got less than one. The bedrooms that face the street at four in the afternoon, the long windowless halls and the caretaker's kitchen came out dim. The car park had no lamp at all: it had been counted as outdoors, so the parked cars, painted near-black, stood in a cave as black blocks.

Now a room's lamps are sized by its floor, six watts a square metre at full power (ten made the halls flat and over-lit), and a new rule, light.room-lamps, checks every room a walk looks into that daylight can't reach. The car park is lit like a room. With the brighter lamps the exposure came down a little, so the patches of low sun on the plaster keep their warm colour instead of burning to flat white.

Rules that look at the pictures and the model

Each key picture is now measured: its mean brightness must sit in a band, with little near-black and little burnt to white (light.exposure). A picture that faces a bare wall close by is replaced by a better moment in the same room (walk.bare-wall); about a fifth of the first pictures were walls at arm's length. And the 3D model is checked for what the eye caught: a piece with no real material, a store's shelves dressed as a library, a curtained store with no curtain, a sunshade or shutters in the window data but not on the building, fruit lying loose on a table. All the numbers are in the owner's rules file.

What was fixed in the model

The sunshade over the big home's yard windows is built. Shutters with no wall to fold against now stand open at the jamb. The store under the big home's stair holds jars, boxes and folded linen behind a curtain that steps down under the flight, and the pantry upstairs has its curtain on its track; so do the other curtained stores. The cars are cars. The roof's drying trays have a rim, and the tomato halves lie in rows. The fine stripes on the wall beside the private stair were its balusters standing 6 cm off the partition that now closes the space under the upper flight: that side takes a wall rail instead. The shared walk now steps into the roof's sunny yard, and the walk of the court level starts and ends where it can see the court.

Left for the owner

Four things in the pictures are design, not modelling, and are cards with a recommendation: housing the fridges that stand out of their runs, a closed panel and a night light where D's bed faces the stairwell, a presence for the street door beside the car opening, and high windows for the roof's raised bay. No floor changed.
