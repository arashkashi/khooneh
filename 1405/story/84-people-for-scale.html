84. People for scale

22 November 2026

The owner, looking at the walks and stills: "if you could in the 3d models put some people so we have a sense of space with people in the space." An empty room hides its size. A ceiling of 2.9 m or a passage of 1.85 m are only numbers until someone stands in them.

Figures, not characters

An architect's model uses scale figures: plain, all one colour, no faces or clothes, so the eye reads the person's height and not who they are. The figures here are built from a stick skeleton scaled to each one's height: a head, a neck, a tapered body, rounded arms and legs and two feet, in one soft matte warm grey. Adults run from 1.55 to 1.85 m, there is a child of 1.1 m, and the elders stand a little stooped. They stand, walk, lean on a counter or sit. Nothing was downloaded: every figure is made from numbers.

Planned before they are built

As with the furniture, every figure is placed in the numbers first and drawn on the plans before it reaches the 3D model. Two or three sit on each main living room's conversation circle, one stands at each kitchen counter with forearms on the worktop, one sits at a dining table on a chair the table has, one walks along the big home's new top-floor passage, a child stands in its play hall, one waits in the lobby by the lift and one works at the roof garden's potting bench: twenty in all.

Three hard rules keep them honest. A figure stands off every door's swing and the floor each side of a door, cuts no way the rules keep open, stands in no furniture, wall or column, and keeps half a metre from every walk's camera line, so no walk passes through a person (PE-place). A seated figure sits only on a real seat, at that seat's height (PE-seat). A room holds at most three, and a switch takes them all away (PE-count). Each number can be tuned. The 2D-3D match builds every figure as the walk does and checks it stands where the plan draws it.

What did not fit

The lobby is 1.65 m wide and the shared walk runs down its middle, so its one figure waits in the bay beside the lift. The big home's soffeh stays empty: its places lie on the walk's camera line. Both are open for the owner to see in the pictures.
