The story

70. What are we missing?

21 November 2026

The owner stepped back and asked: "I wonder what are we missing? we started from the ground up the pillars and then rooms and then the interiors, I would want to see what are we missing in this direction." They also asked for a little doubt, for the empty spaces where nothing had been decided, and for a few experiments run through the pipeline.

Walking up the building again

The design was walked again in its own order: the site, the structure, the core and its services, the envelope, the rooms, the interiors, life in use, and building and running it. A small program searched the project's own rules, data, cards and notes for 49 topics and graded each one by what really exists. Fourteen are measured by a rule. Eleven are measured only in part, eleven are decided, six are described in words only, six appear only in notes, and one has nothing at all.

The rooms, the furniture, the kitchens, the baths and the daylight are measured more carefully than in most real projects. What is thin is the layer in between: what keeps the building warm, dry, supplied and buildable. No wall, roof or floor has any insulation written down. Nobody had worked out how much heat each room needs. Hot water had no design, and three notes said three different things about it. There was no water tank and no pump to raise the pressure. There was no earthing, and that one goes into the foundations first. There were no quantities at all, so there was no way to price anything.

A floor too thin for what it holds

The sharpest finding came from a doubt about something everyone had taken for granted: the floors. Every floor is 180 mm from the concrete to the surface you walk on. The drain rule let every drain use 140 mm of it. But the same 180 mm also holds the floating screed that stops footsteps reaching the home below, and a pipe can't run inside that. Told the honest depth, about 70 mm, the drains of ten wet rooms no longer fall to their riser.

Two cures were tried in the pipeline. Making every floor deeper broke three other rules at once: nine yard windows would meet the beams, a kitchen cupboard would meet the ceiling, and the car park would lose headroom. Sinking only the ten short wet rooms, each by the few centimetres it lacks, passed everything. The fault is local, and so is its cure. The lesson goes beyond drains: when several things share one thickness, they should all read their share from one record, not each guess it.

The heat, room by room

A second experiment worked out every room's heat need from its walls, windows, roof and floor. With the insulation the window studies had assumed, the whole building needs about 20 kW, and about 37 kW with a hot-water store to reheat. With no insulation, the way many buildings are still built, it needs about 35 kW and 53 kW. The 60 kW boiler in the services is the size an uninsulated building wants. Floor heating would warm every room except the big home's kitchen and coat nook, but only if the walls are insulated. Insulating inside instead of outside costs the homes 6.8 m² between them and still loses 10 to 18% more heat, because every floor slab carries the cold through the wall.

Doubts that held, and doubts that didn't

Several of the design's big choices stood up to measuring. The core belongs where it is. Who lives on which floor is fixed by the sizes of the homes: only one of 48 patterns works. The floating floors keep the homes quiet even under a stricter rule. Other doubts held. The street is 8 m wide, and in Tehran that would cap the storeys at 3.40 m, which the top floor and the shared basement exceed. The lowest level, the plant room and stores two storeys down, is more than half of all the digging. And the court is worth its cost only as long as the shared basement it lights is.

What comes next

Nothing in the design was changed. Other branches are working on it now. When they have merged, six small steps are ready: the honest drain rule with the ten sunk wet rooms; the envelope written down as data, insulated outside; the heat worked out by the pipeline, sizing the boiler and the floor heating; a place for every missing service; the quantities kept as a record; and three questions for the owner and the engineer about the lowest level's price, the storey rule on an 8 m street, and the contractor's prices.
