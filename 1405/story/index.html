The Khooneh story

How the design is taking shape: the significant points, decisions and changes of mind, written as they happen. This folder is private and isn't published.

The form.
The owner's local copy, on this computer.

    A tooling question becomes a pipeline of computable design files.

  2. 2. Values before options

    The options are paused, 1399 is read again, and values turn into specs.

  3. 3. The first full run

    Both designs go through every phase, and the decisions land in one form.

  4. 4. If the AI answered

    A bilingual form, a hypothetical design from the AI's own answers, and a critic who found its faults.

  5. 5. The midnight review

    A second check finds a court stair nobody could reach and numbers left over from the first draft.

  6. 6. The plot turns

    Two real plots face north-east, and a sun study shows the rear neighbour matters most.

  7. 7. How we use AI to design

    What the AI does, what people do, where it went wrong and what it can't do.

  8. 8. Two quiet teachers

    Zumthor and Amanat enter the form as ways of thinking, and the form opens up to newcomers.

  9. 9. The form leaves home

    The form goes out to other people by private link, and the question of how to judge the design begins.

  10. 10. Moving the columns

    The owner notices the draft has been following old columns, and the 1403 design, modelled at last, shows another way.

  11. 11. The owner answers

    The first full set of answers settles a good deal, and brings the high room back into question.

  12. 12. How we design

    The process gets a name: eight stages, who decides what, and when details get fixed.

  13. 13. The first AI version

    Research settles what the answers left open, a critic tears up the first draft, and the design goes all the way to 3D.

  14. 14. The form stays open

    The form stays available for new people, and gains a button for starting afresh on a shared browser.

  15. 15. The rooms become numbers

    Walls, doors and furniture go on the plan, floor living is measured, and an optimiser shows what doors can fix and what they can't.

  16. 16. The plan becomes ordinary

    The owner's critique becomes rules, two respected designs calibrate them, and iteration 3 is redrawn as a plain apartment plan.

  17. 17. The site speaks

    A new stack for iteration 4, front doors that swing inward, and a confirmed site that puts the sun at the top of the building, and a shaded court that waits for a tall tree.

  18. 18. Four homes, not five

    Iteration 4 is drawn: a caretaker on F2, two duplexes, a spiral stair, a laundry route and a basement that looks onto the tree.

  19. 19. Measure before searching

    Optimisation learns to check whether it is worth running, two tools point at the same balcony, and the court gets its upright birch.

  20. 20. Walking through

    Films of every home in late October light, with the seasons at the window; what watching them showed, and a wider view.

  21. 21. The court comes in

    The green strip on the owner's 1399 drawing becomes a garden inside −1, in 1399 and in 1405: first a porch, then, as the owner clarified, a symmetric garden pocket behind glass on the tree's axis.

  22. 22. Pictures before films

    Walks are checked as preview pictures before any film is rendered; the owner's walk rules become a checker; one fixed October light; the −1 garden reaches into the house behind glass.

  23. 23. A kitchen to cook in

    Proposed, awaiting the owner: a preparation island with the sink facing the room, the hob on a solid wall, the fridge away from the glass, a table beside it, the TV at right angles to the windows; every kitchen sized to its household and checked by eight new rules.

  24. 24. Who lives here

    Fictional households for every home, and a day in each checked against the plan: no queue on a school morning, but a sofa that reaches no home, an open stair for a toddler, a duplex with its only WC upstairs, and laundry carried between floors.

  25. 25. A shower beside the tub

    Done, awaiting the owner: every bath with a tub also gets a separate stand-up shower, checked by a new rule; the big home's top-floor bath takes the strip the narrower stair gave and a moved door; the first-floor home keeps its level shower, with a tub as the owner's option.

  26. 26. A kitchen that grows with the home, and a place to sit

    Done, awaiting the owner: the big home's kitchen 1.0 m bigger with its island inside it; a 0.40 m soffeh with drawers at the living room's east end; seven rules that keep the homes' proportions.

  27. 27. A roof that feeds the house

    Done, awaiting the owner and the engineer: the roof becomes a kitchen garden, a plot for every home and shared sabzi beds, a harvest station with a sink, vines, fruit trees and a windbreak; the cooling units move to the stair house; three rules for plots, water and load.

  28. 28. Windows for the seasons

    Done, awaiting the owner: with a building our size 6 m behind the yard, only the fourth floor gets winter sun; lower yard sills rise to 0.9 m, two balconies move off the windows below, and nine window rules.

  29. 29. Decisions on one page

    The cards are tidied, every open question gets its own page with options drawn left and right, a morph and a wipe, the numbers and the AI's answer; and the owner asks for a rule on small spaces.

  30. 30. Pictures that follow rules

    Every picture of 1405 looked at again: camera rules the owner can tune, composed room pictures with upright walls, and the design points the pictures raised turned into questions.

  31. 31. Numbers before pictures

    The owner turns the order the right way round: detailed furniture and 2D drawings of every floor first, furniture that knows its neighbours, rooms that may grow for a reason, services from the layout, the work in layers, and creative risers.

  32. 32. Putting the numbers in order

    The numerical pipeline tidied under a safety net that builds the design twice for every step: render code out of the numbers, one place for each thing, the programme as data, the old author archived, and the numbers that disagree left for the owner.

  33. 33. A bed out of sight

    Privacy made a rule, P-bed-sight, before any option for the small duplex's upper floor: six ways measured in full, the owner's corner idea among them, and the bedroom's door moved up the hall so the loft opens without the bed being seen.

  34. 34. No floor left dead

    The owner finds two dead gaps in the caretaker's bath; four rules for every room (no dead strip, flush or roomy, little dead floor, fill what can't be closed), the search placing by them, and dead floor across the building from 7.1 to 1.8 m².

  35. 35. The kitchen at work

    Every kitchen as numbers: what is kept where, how the sink, the hob and the table relate, and fourteen everyday scenarios walked and scored, with the everyday table sized for the people who eat together.

  36. 36. Where the sky runs out

    The owner asks what to do where windows see almost no sky. The rows were an older design's; in 1405 each lost point is traced to what takes it, twelve fixes are measured, a new rule checks every window, and one dark bedroom window is the owner's to decide.

  37. 37. A bath on the terrace

    The owner moves B's master bath onto the top floor's street terrace: a bath of 8.6 m² with a door from the couple's room, its costs in permit area, drains and slab measured, and the old bath turned into the pantry B never had.

  38. 38. A machine for every home

    The owner asks for a washer, a dishwasher, a fridge and a freezer in every home, sized to it and a step larger than the industry's standard: every home gets its dishwasher, the larger homes two columns side by side, and the caretaker's small kitchen becomes a question.

  39. 39. Where the pipes go

    Awaiting the owner and the engineer: what a better riser position is, as seven rules and their weights; R3 too shallow for its pipes, so D's guest WC needs it 5 cm deeper; three risers stay the right number; R2 slimmer as the owner's option; every riser and shaft labelled on every plan.

  40. 42. A tub and a shower

    The owner asks for a tub and a stand-up shower in A's bath, the corners used; a room optimiser in tiers (fixtures, light walls, the riser, the columns last) fits both without moving a wall, and shows what a new riser would cure; D's bath measured the same way.

  41. 40. A concrete frame

    The building in reinforced concrete, sized on the safe side for Qazvin's very high earthquake hazard: shear walls on the party walls and round the core, the beams, slabs and mats, and five questions for the owner and the engineer.

  42. 41. Stairs by the numbers

    One register of every stair and step, fourteen rules over it, each floor height split into equal risers, and two cards: D's spiral treads and the core box's width.

  43. 43. Pieces that belong together

    Furniture relations as numbers: chairs that can be pulled out, a TV in front of the sofa, ways that stay wide, no wardrobe across a window, and the caretaker's tub pocket explained and closed.

  44. 44. The round of 17 November

    The day's work joined into one design: the owner's answer on small rooms (every room with a bed at least 10 m², small open spaces marked), the scores before and after, progress for the tests, and the questions that wait.

  45. 45. An architect looks at 1405

    An independent review by an architect in Qazvin: a Persian house carried into today, sound in its rooms. Eight proposals for what is missing (a protected stair, sections and elevations, gas, meters and heating, terraces over rooms, a hashti screen, a level roof door, a stretcher lift, quiet floors), and three fixes made at once.

  46. 46. Decisions taken for you

    The owner delegated every waiting question to the architect: higher storeys, stairs drawn as they are, B's bath on the terrace, D's open loft, a stronger frame, and a list of every choice with the way to change it.

  47. 47. The empty shaft

    The night-cooling shaft beside the lift was an empty box. Measured, it would cool little and leak heat and smoke between homes, so it is closed into a sealed box on each floor and the roof corner goes back to the garden.

  48. 48. The review applied

    The independent review applied: a fire door and closers keep the stair a way out, a permit set drawn from the data, meters, gas and one boiler, terraces with no step, a lattice hashti at two front doors, a level roof door, quiet floors; the stretcher lift did not fit and is recorded why.

  49. 49. AI decisions, kept open

    The 65 decisions the AI took for you are marked as AI decisions and stay open for your review, built into the design; checked against your own words, two were changed: walks end where they started, and the −1 floor's long wall stays plain.

  50. 50. The caretaker's kitchen

    Two hobs drawn where the data had one, a fridge door into a cupboard and a shut slot behind the stove: each found in the numbers, made a rule (one cooktop, doors that open fully, no floor shut behind a run), and the kitchen search rebuilt every home's kitchen by them.

  51. 51. The free middle

    Level −1 as the owner asked: the tea corner in the south-west corner under the court glass, the toilet against the old shaft with its fan duct up the closed box, one free floor from the gym to the shared room, and −1 as high as the rules allow: 3.60 m from floor to floor, 3.20 m clear for a gym.

  52. 52. The door that faces the hall

    The owner's feedback, that a front door should face the living space and not the bedrooms and baths, made a soft rule (P-entry-view) that measures the first view from inside each front door; the step-free home failed, and its front door moved south to face the family hall and the lift.

  53. 53. The island at the edge

    The owner's yes to an island kitchen in the step-free home: the island stands between the living room's two columns, 1.55 m from the wall run so a wheelchair turns, dry, with a seated place at its south end; a hard rule (KZ-step-free) asks every step-free kitchen for a seated place and a wheelchair's aisle beside an island.

  54. 54. A table for eight

    The owner's extending table for the small duplex, four every day and eight for guests: a new table in the library, a guest check that measures the open table, its chairs and the way past them; four seated every day, the guest night still fails and says why; the spiral stays; the structure is kept as it is while an initial analysis is made. Corrected the same day: the duplex never has eight, so four sit at its everyday table for four, and its front doors widen from 0.72 to 0.87 m clear.

  55. 55. The big home's door

    The owner's idea, a front door like the small duplex's: on the grandparents' floor it lost (a wheelchair could not turn on the landing); on the big home's living floor it won by a little. The door opens onto the living room, the sofa gets in, the guest WC sits just inside, the lattice goes.

  56. 56. A lived-in home

    A little more furniture, measured: rugs, bookcases, plants and reading lamps where they fit, every way kept and no room over 40% furniture; the big home's street corner becomes the children's homework corner, ready to become the youngest's room later.

  57. 57. The box given back

    The closed box beside the lift, about 19 m² on six levels, is gone: the shear walls stay, the riser moves to the party wall, and the strip becomes a store, a pram bay, a level shower room, a work nook, a linen store and a coat bay; the night-cooling idea is retired.
