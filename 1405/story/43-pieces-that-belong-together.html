Why the tub pocket came back

Nothing was stale. The search had read the dead-floor rule too literally. Floor counted as usable when a 0.30 m square could reach it from the door, and 0.30 m is the rule's smallest strip. So the search slid the tub 0.30 m off the north wall: a 0.30 m slot opened at its head, and through that slot the 0.5 x 0.85 m notch behind it counted as "reached". No one stands in a slot. Now usable floor must lie within a hand's and a mop's reach, 1.0 m along the floor, of somewhere a person can stand (a 0.45 m square joined to the door). The tub may also meet the column with part of its front, because you step into a tub from the middle of its long side. The search now lays the tub along the north wall with its head in the notch. What is left is a 0.1 m strip under the tub's apron.

The chairs on the walls

The decision pages drew a table's chairs if they fell inside any room of the floor, so the hall table showed two chairs across the bath wall and the hob run. design-1405.html already refused those chairs. Now the search records the chairs a table really has, and every page draws only those.

The new numbers

  • F-overlap: no piece, use floor or chair stands on another piece, a wall, a column, a stair or a door's swing, here or in the room next door.
  • KZ-table-clear, extended: every chair of every table keeps 0.6 m (your 0.6 to 0.75) behind it.
  • F-tv: the TV within 0.3 m and 15° of the sofa's axis, 2.0 to 3.6 m away for a 55" screen, its back on a solid wall for 90 % of its width, and not in a door's approach. T1 gains a glare cone: no window within 20° of the screen, seen from the sofa.
  • LV-view: between the sofa and the TV nothing higher than a coffee table. LV-group: one seating group. LV-zone: dining and living apart, and no way through the view. LV-sofa-back: the sofa's back on a wall, or 0.9 m of way behind it.
  • F-pair: a coffee table 0.4 to 0.5 m from the sofa, night tables at the bed's head, 0.75 m for a desk's chair. The table and the serve landing stay rule KZ-hob-table.
  • PS-route: 1.0 m ways through halls, living rooms and entries, 0.9 m through bedrooms, between every two ways in and from each to the table and the sofa, counting the chairs pulled out and the doors' swings. PS-table-room: a table only where it, its chairs pulled out and that way fit.
  • F8-block (hard): no tall piece covers more than 20 % of a window's glass. F8-window also keeps a low piece's reach to the handle and keeps a wardrobe's doors out of a casement's swing.

The search uses the same numbers it is judged by. A table now carries its chairs and their pull-out floor instead of a ring. A TV is only placed with its back on a wall. A sofa may stand free, but only facing a place the TV can go and with a way behind it. The TV, the coffee table and an armchair are placed for their relation to the sofa.
