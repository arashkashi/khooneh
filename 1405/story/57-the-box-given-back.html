The story

57. The box given back

19 November 2026

Beside the lift, on six levels, stood a closed box: what was left of the night-cooling shaft once it was found to cool little and lose heat. It had no way in and no use, about 19 m² in all, and nothing in the checks noticed it. You wrote: "unbelievable, you have lost about 19 square meter with the closed air shaft, if it is wasting heat, then it should be removed. I know it is a costly thing but maybe we need to do what we need to do." And: "bring back the 19 square meter to the floors please." And: "make sure there are enough configs and rules which would make it a meaningful space, and not a dead little space which could not be used."

The walls stay

The box sat between two concrete walls that hold the building against earthquakes across the plot. Taking the south one away was measured first, with the preliminary seismic model: the building would sway more than it may, even with the other wall made thicker. So both walls stay. The supply riser moves to the box's far end, against the party wall, and the floor it and the box leave opens west, where no wall or column stands, to the room beside the core on each level.

What each floor gains

On the lowest shared level, a store for the table tennis table, the stacked chairs and the gym's mats. On the ground floor, a bay off the walkway for prams, a wheelchair and the caretaker's trolley. In the grandparents' home, the WC by the entrance becomes a shower room, level throughout, with room to turn a wheelchair. In the small duplex, a work nook off the entry downstairs and a linen store off the upper entry. In the big home's living floor, whose WC had already moved with its new front door the same day, the strip becomes the entry's coat and shoe bay. About 15 m² of clear floor come back.

The cooling idea, retired

You also asked to "clean up the part of the specs for a vertical tunnel for cooling etc. apparently it is not possible to do, or you can not integrate that idea into the modern building like this". The idea is dropped from the specs. Its physics and its winter check are kept in the archive for reference. The lower wet room's air now goes through the wall into the supply riser and up to the roof.

No dead little spaces

A new check looks at every pocket of a room like these: it must open wide enough, not reach back too deep, and, if deep, hold something. Together with the floor audit, which now accounts for every square metre of every level, a closed box like this one can no longer slip through.
