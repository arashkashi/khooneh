A house in Qazvin, still unbuilt

How do we live well, together?

A family, a two-hundred-square-metre plot in Qazvin, seven years, two designs by one architect, and still no house. Here we tell the story as it happened — the list we wrote ourselves, the drawings that were made, the questions still open — and look for people who would like to continue the conversation with us: how, on an ordinary city plot, three generations, a guest and a caretaker might live near one another, share a garden and a roof, and stay good neighbours to the street.

The story so far

  1. 2019

    We started with a list.

    My parents, my family and I wrote down what we wanted from a house, in our own words rather than a designer's: nineteen points, from the size of the bathroom to a staircase you climb like a small hill. The list is below, unedited.

  2. 2020

    The first design.

    An architect in Qazvin drew a building for a 10.55 × 19 m plot. His first presentation was a conventional stack of flats and we turned it down; the second interwove the units in height — a first floor 5.52 m tall with a loft hung inside it, a sunken courtyard with a real tree, a parking floor screened by a lattice and a green wall. The municipality approved it. Then the plot fell through, and the approval went with it.

  3. 2020–21

    The same drawings, a second plot.

    The plans were re-fitted to another site, with two sets intended: one for the municipality, one to build from. It never went further. In the architect's words later, everything was ready; we were not.

  4. 2024

    The third attempt.

    A 10.50 × 20.14 m plot on Mollasadra Street, the same architect, and a new premise: instead of fighting the building code, breathe the spirit of the first design into it. Six levels, four units, three of them duplexes, eight parking spaces if pushed. Buildable.

  5. 2024–25

    We did not converge.

    I wrote thirty-four pages of observations; the architect wrote three reports back. We disagreed about what the building was for as much as about where the stair should go. In spring 2025 I stepped away, with the friendship intact and the design unbuilt.

  6. 2026

    This page.

    The plot, the brief and both sets of drawings are here for anyone to read. The question is still open, and I would rather open it up than keep asking it alone.

The plot and the rules

Plot
10.50 × 20.14 m, about 211 m², on the 38th alley off Mollasadra Street, Qazvin. Street to the north, yard to the south.
Buildable
60 % coverage plus a 2 m legal projection: roughly 140 m² per floor plate, of which the stair and lift core takes about 22.
Height
The street is caught between three- and four-floor zoning. Both neighbours are already built more than a floor over the limit, and they shadow the plot.
Noise
The alley leads to a newly cut north–south boulevard used as a short-cut. Traffic and noise will grow, not shrink.
Neighbours
Stone cladding, classical columns, heavy ornament. A simple façade will have to hold its own beside them.
Climate
Qazvin sits at about 1,300 m: cold winters, hot dry summers, big swings between day and night. The brief's insistence on natural ventilation without street noise comes from living here.
Money
Prices in rials move too fast to plan with. We want a design we can cost in tonnes and cubic metres.

The brief, in the family's words

We wrote this in 2019, before any architect was involved, and it has held up. It is the closest thing to a specification the project has. The Persian is the original; the English is my translation.

  1. اهمیت آشپزخانه به عنوان فضایی که خیلی زمان زیادی در اون صرف میشه، و دسترسی آسان به اتاق نشیمن، راحتی حرکت و چیدمان وسایل و نور طبیعی. شاید تلفیق نشیمن و آشپزخانه؟؟

    The kitchen is where the most time is spent: easy reach to the living room, ease of movement and layout, natural light. Perhaps merge living and kitchen?

  2. اهمیت و راحتی ضیافت شام در منزل و دور همی های دوستانه و خانوادگی باظرفیت ۸ نفر البته با -۸ شامل شدن ساکنین خانه، دسترسی سریع و آسان به اشپزخانه برای پذیرایی و نظافت. در صورت امکان فراهم کردن گوشه دنج برای صحبتهای ۲ نفره.

    Dinner for eight, residents included, with quick access to the kitchen for serving and clearing. If possible, a snug corner for a conversation between two people.

  3. اتاق کار با میز کار با کاربری دوگانه اتاق مهمان همراه با دسترسی به نور طبیعی و فضا برای آرشیو کردن مدارک کاری و زندگی. اتاق کار نیاز به فضای بزرگی ندارد.

    A small study with a desk that doubles as a guest room, with daylight and room to archive the papers of work and life. It need not be large.

  4. سرویس بهداشتی بزرگتر از اندازه های رایج (به نظر در طرحهایی که من دیدم از لحاظ مقدار تخصیص فضا به سرویس بهداشتی کم فروشی شده) همراه با وان و دوش ایستاده مجزا. در مورد سرویس بهداشتی دوم با توجه به کمبود فضا اولویت شاید بالا نباشد. من شخصا از وجود نور طبیعی در‌ سرویس بهداشتی لذت می برم در صورت امکان و صلاحدید وجود پنجره در سرویس بهداشتی

    A bathroom larger than the norm — most plans I have seen short-change it — with a bath and a separate standing shower. A second bathroom is low priority given the space. A window in the bathroom if at all possible; I enjoy daylight there.

  5. جمعا سه اتاق همراه اتاق کار/مهمان. دو اتاق دیگر به عنوان خواب برای پدر و مادر و یک اتاق خواب فرزند. اگر‌امکان تغییر طراحی در طبقات مختلف باشه برای خانواده بازنشسته (پدر و‌مادر بنده) جمعا دو خواب کافی میباشد، و برای دو خانوار دیگر دو خواب یکی برای اولیا و یک خواب برای فرزند.

    Three rooms in total including the study: two bedrooms for the parents, one for a child. If the floors can differ, the retired household — my parents — needs two bedrooms, and each of the other two households needs two: parents and child.

  6. واحد سرایدار ساکن در ساختمان شامل اشپزخانه یک حال و یک خواب.

    A resident caretaker's unit: kitchen, a living room, one bedroom.

  7. ایجاد فضای کوچکی برای عنوان محیط ورزش برای ۳-۴ نفر صرفا برای حرکتهای کششی و ورزشهای طبی بدون نیاز فضا به ابزار مخصوص و مکانیکی همچنین یک حوضچه کوچک برای نشستن ۲-۳ نفر مجهز به جت برای اب گرم/سرد برای تقویت عروق و در‌صورت امکان فضا سونای خشک چوبی یک‌نفره، این فضا‌عمومی برای تمامی اهالی ساختمان می باشد.

    A small shared wellness room for three or four people: stretching and therapeutic movement, no machines; a small sitting pool for two or three with hot and cold jets; if space allows, a one-person dry wooden sauna. Shared by everyone in the building.

  8. ایجاد فضای سبز موثر و خلاق در فضای محدود حیاط.

    Effective, inventive greenery in the tight courtyard.

  9. روف گاردن دارای قابلیت کشت سبزیجات و فضای سبز قابلیت دسترسی تمام طبقات.

    A roof garden where vegetables can grow, reachable from every floor.

  10. انباری متناسب برای واحدها.

    Adequate storage for each unit.

  11. از طرفی تمایل زیاد به پنجره بزرگ. و دریافت نور زیاد و از طرفی به علت فرم کوچه و خ. عملا پنجره اکثرا پشت پرده می ماند، همچنان امیدوار به راه حل این تناقض؟

    On one hand a strong wish for big windows and plenty of light; on the other, because of the alley and the street, windows mostly end up behind curtains. I still hope for a solution to this contradiction.

  12. نما ساده و مقاوم به گذر زمان یک نمونه در عکسها براتون ارسال می کنم.

    A simple façade that ages well.

  13. با توجه به صحبتهای انجام شده امکان ایجاد فضا در طبقه ۱- در صورت نیاز ممکن است. وحتی مهنای ۲.

    Space at level −1 is possible if needed — and even at level −2.

  14. امکان تنفس طبیعی ساختمان بدون ورود سر و صدای بیرون از خانه.

    The building should breathe naturally without letting the noise of the street in.

  15. سادگی و هزینه کم اجرا

    Simplicity and low cost of construction.

  16. راه کار تهویه و چرخش طبیعی هوا از طریق معابر و پنجره های باز همزمان در فضای شمالی و جنوبی

    Natural ventilation and air movement through passages and windows open at the same time on the north and south sides.

  17. قابلیت ارزیابی مقدار مصالح مورد نیاز نه به ریال بلکه به مقدار

    The ability to estimate the materials needed — in quantities, not in rials.

  18. راه پله دارای پنجره فضای مورد استفاده برای بازی و نشستن صعود و نزول از تپه کوچک راه پله بزرگ ولی فضا کم بگیره :) پله های تو در تو

    A staircase with a window, a place to sit and play, the climb of a small hill. A generous stair that takes little room :) — nested stairs.

  19. اسانسور بزرگتر از حدود استاندارد اسان برای حمل بار و مقاوم در مقابل overload

    A lift larger than standard, easy for moving loads, and robust against overload.

What the list does not say

Behind the nineteen points is a household arrangement: a unit for my parents, a unit for a large family, a small unit for a student or a guest, a caretaker, and one more unit in mind for the basement. Behind that is something harder to write as a requirement. We wanted a tree — a real one, in the ground. We wanted a second basement, not for storage, but as a room the neighbourhood could use: somewhere to exercise, to sit in water, to gather. We wanted a house that could, over its life, turn into something else — a small cultural room, a place that serves more than one social role — without being rebuilt.

Who are we going to be, and how is this space going to change the way we meet the rest of the world?

Nobody has answered that yet, including us. It is the reason this page exists.

Attempt I — 2020

The design I still think about.

The plot was 10.55 by 19 metres. The architect gave up the idea of a building as a stack of identical floors and worked in height instead. The first floor is 5.52 m tall, with a loft hung over its north half: a library, a bedroom and a room with its own exhaust for smoking guests. Below, the parking floor is separated from the courtyard by a مشبک — a lattice screen — and a green wall, so that the car is not the first thing the house shows you. The courtyard itself drops a full storey to the basement, where the caretaker lives, where there is a small pool for sitting in, and where a tree grows up through the yard.

On the sheets nothing is labelled as a unit, but the arrangement we discussed was three: a student or guest unit of about 60 m² across half of two floors, a family duplex of about 110, and the main household with about 190 — plus a conventional flat on the top floor for my parents, and the basement below. The municipality approved these plans before the plot was lost.

  • First floor 5.52 m clear, loft at +6.08, second and third floors 3.30 m clear
  • Shabak screen and green wall between the cars and the yard
  • Opaque glass floor over the courtyard void, so the yard stays walkable while the basement gets light
  • Balcony with a sliding floor and folding rail; flower box along the street console
  • Caretaker unit, jacuzzi, shower and locker room in the basement

Attempt III — 2024

The design that could be built.

Four years later, on the Mollasadra plot, the same architect started from the code: 60 % coverage, a 2 m projection, a stair core that eats 22 m² of every floor, and a street that might be zoned for three floors or four. His answer was to reach the floor area we needed by using the third dimension — three of the four units are duplexes — and to make the second floor independent of the lift so that the same plans pass under either zoning.

Three ideas in it deserve credit. The ایوان — the iwan — returned as a half-open room between inside and outside, which the architect argued had been lost when Iranian housing replaced it with "the incomplete notion of the terrace". The گودال باغچه, the sunken garden, came back not as a private feature but as the building's commons, a place for the four households to become neighbours; he had a word for it, باهمستان. And after I asked, the swimming pool in the basement became a حوض: a traditional tiled pool to sit in, with a dry sauna beside it.

The architect saw in these drawings the spirit of the 2020 design carried through the code. I saw a competent building that no longer said anything new to me. Both readings are on the table; neither is the last word.

What remains open

These are the questions neither attempt answered. They are the reason to write to us.

  1. Level −2

    A second basement as a shared room — for movement, water, gathering — seven metres under a Qazvin alley. What does it need in light, air, egress, water and cost, and is it worth it?

  2. The window paradox

    Big windows and lots of light, but privacy from a narrow street. Lattice, double skin, iwan, deep reveals, courtyard-facing glass — which of these works at 200 m², and at what cost?

  3. Breathing without noise

    North–south cross ventilation in a building beside a boulevard that will only get louder.

  4. Seeing both sides at once

    A spatial quality I keep asking for and cannot draw: standing in the main room and seeing the north and the south windows together.

  5. A house that can become a room for others

    Convertibility over decades — from three households to a small cultural space or a shared workplace — without rebuilding.

  6. Cost in quantities

    A design that can be estimated in tonnes of steel and cubic metres of concrete, so that a currency collapse does not collapse the project.

  7. The tree

    A real tree in a sunken courtyard: the opening in the foundation, the roots, the light, the species.

  8. The roof

    A garden that every household can reach and tend, or a private terrace for one — and what that choice says about the building.

  9. The mix

    Student or guest, retired parents, a large family, a caretaker, on 200 m² and four or five floors. Duplexes or flat floors? Which unit gets the ground?

  10. A façade that ages

    Simple, durable, and at ease among stone-clad neighbours that are trying very hard.

The invitation

We are not looking for a design to buy. We are looking for people who find the same problem worth solving — the ordinary Iranian city plot of two hundred square metres, and what a building on it could be if energy, climate, cost, neighbourhood and the way a family actually lives were all taken seriously at once. If the answer we find together can be reused on the next plot, and the one after, so much the better.

What we bring

  • A plot in Qazvin and a family that has been thinking about this since 2019
  • A written brief, and two complete sets of drawings to learn from
  • Patience for iteration, and a real budget for a real building

What we ask

  • Architects — and engineers, landscape and energy people — who want to work in the open, with the questions above as the starting point
  • Short conversations, often; written decisions; an agreed rhythm for reviews
  • Agreement on the whole before the details, and clarity about who decides what
